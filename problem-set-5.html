PROBLEM SET 5

Exercise 1

VAR stability

Are the following VAR processes stable? Write the companion matrix where needed. Roots may be reported numerically.

  1. \(Y_t\) such that
    \[Y_t=\begin{pmatrix}0.5&0.1\\0&0.2\end{pmatrix}Y_{t-1}+\varepsilon_t.\]
  2. \(Z_t\) such that
    \[\begin{aligned}Z_t={}&\begin{pmatrix}0.6&0.4\\0.5&0.2\end{pmatrix}Z_{t-1}\\&+\begin{pmatrix}0.1&0.3\\0.2&0.6\end{pmatrix}Z_{t-2}+\eta_t.\end{aligned}\]

Question 1Stability of the VAR(1)

Formulas to use — Question 1

Find the eigenvalues: solve the characteristic equation of the coefficient matrix.

\[\det(\lambda I-\Phi)=0.\]

Check stability: every eigenvalue must have modulus below 1.

\[\rho(\Phi)=\max_i|\lambda_i|<1.\]

For a triangular matrix, the eigenvalues are its diagonal entries.

Solution

This is already a VAR(1), so use its coefficient matrix directly:

\[\Phi=\begin{pmatrix}0.5&0.1\\0&0.2\end{pmatrix}.\]
\[\begin{aligned}\det(\lambda I_2-\Phi)&=\begin{vmatrix}\lambda-0.5&-0.1\\0&\lambda-0.2\end{vmatrix}\\&=(\lambda-0.5)(\lambda-0.2)\\&=\lambda^2-0.7\lambda+0.1.\end{aligned}\]

Thus \(\lambda_1=0.5\) and \(\lambda_2=0.2\). Both are inside the unit circle:

\[\boxed{\rho(\Phi)=0.5<1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\text{the VAR(1) is stable.}}\]

Question 2Companion matrix and stability of the VAR(2)

Formulas to use — Question 2

Stack the current and lagged vectors: rewrite the VAR(2) as a VAR(1).

\[W_t=\begin{pmatrix}Z_t\\Z_{t-1}\end{pmatrix},\qquad F=\begin{pmatrix}\Phi_1&\Phi_2\\I_2&0\end{pmatrix}.\]

Apply the same stability test to \(F\):

\[\det(\lambda I_4-F)=0,\qquad\rho(F)=\max_i|\lambda_i|<1.\]

For a determinant expanded down column \(k\), use \(\det A=\sum_i a_{ik}C_{ik}\), where \(C_{ik}=(-1)^{i+k}M_{ik}\) is the cofactor.

Solution

Let \(W_t=(Z_t',Z_{t-1}')'\). The top block reproduces the model; the bottom block carries \(Z_{t-1}\) into the state:

\[W_t=FW_{t-1}+\begin{pmatrix}\eta_t\\0\end{pmatrix}.\]
\[\boxed{F=\begin{pmatrix}\Phi_1&\Phi_2\\I_2&0\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0.6&0.4&0.1&0.3\\0.5&0.2&0.2&0.6\\1&0&0&0\\0&1&0&0\end{pmatrix}.}\]

Expand \(\det(F-\lambda I_4)\) down column 3, whose entries are \(0.1,0.2,-\lambda,0\):

\[\det(F-\lambda I_4)=0.1C_{13}+0.2C_{23}-\lambda C_{33}.\]

Delete the row and column of each entry, then apply the cofactor sign:

\[\begin{aligned}C_{13}&=\begin{vmatrix}0.5&0.2-\lambda&0.6\\1&0&0\\0&1&-\lambda\end{vmatrix}\\&=0.6+0.2\lambda-\lambda^2,\end{aligned}\]
\[\begin{aligned}C_{23}&=-\begin{vmatrix}0.6-\lambda&0.4&0.3\\1&0&0\\0&1&-\lambda\end{vmatrix}\\&=-0.3-0.4\lambda,\end{aligned}\]
\[\begin{aligned}C_{33}&=\begin{vmatrix}0.6-\lambda&0.4&0.3\\0.5&0.2-\lambda&0.6\\0&1&-\lambda\end{vmatrix}\\&=(0.6-\lambda)(\lambda^2-0.2\lambda-0.6)\\&\quad+0.2\lambda+0.15.\end{aligned}\]

Substitute these three expressions into the column expansion and collect powers of \(\lambda\).

Since the matrix is \(4\times4\), \(\det(F-\lambda I_4)=\det(\lambda I_4-F)\). This gives

\[\boxed{\det(\lambda I_4-F)=\lambda^4-0.8\lambda^3-0.78\lambda^2+0.15\lambda.}\]

The eigenvalues are approximately

\[\{-0.67766,\ 0,\ 0.16916,\ 1.30850\}.\]
\[\boxed{\rho(F)\simeq1.30850>1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\text{the VAR(2) is unstable.}}\]
Exercise 2

Main outputs of a VAR process

Consider the following VAR(1), using its causal stationary solution:

\[Y_t=\Phi Y_{t-1}+\varepsilon_t,\qquad\Phi=\begin{pmatrix}0.5&0.1\\0&0.2\end{pmatrix},\]
\[\varepsilon_t\overset{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}\mathcal N(0,\Sigma_\varepsilon),\qquad\Sigma_\varepsilon=\begin{pmatrix}0.3&0.2\\0.2&0.5\end{pmatrix}.\]
  1. Compute the VMA(\(\infty\)) form of \(Y_t\).
  2. Compute the reduced-form IRFs and the long-run cumulative IRFs of \(Y_t\).
  3. Compute the orthogonalised IRFs and their long-run cumulative responses. State the shock normalization used.

Question 1VMA(∞) representation and diagonalization

Formulas to use — Question 1

Invert the stable VAR(1):

\[(I_2-\Phi L)^{-1}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\Phi^jL^j,\qquad Y_t=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\Phi^j\varepsilon_{t-j}.\]

Compute matrix powers by diagonalization: find eigenvectors \(d_i\) from \((\Phi-\lambda_i I_2)d_i=0\), then set \(D=(d_1,d_2)\).

\[\Phi=D\Lambda D^{-1}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\Phi^j=D\Lambda^jD^{-1}.\]

Solution

Stability was established in Exercise 1. Therefore,

\[(I_2-\Phi L)Y_t=\varepsilon_t\quad\Longrightarrow\quad Y_t=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\Phi^j\varepsilon_{t-j}.\]

1. Find the eigenvectors

For \(\lambda_1=0.5\),

\[\begin{pmatrix}0&0.1\\0&-0.3\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}d_{11}\\d_{21}\end{pmatrix}=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad d_{21}=0.\]

Choose \(d_{11}=1\). For \(\lambda_2=0.2\),

\[\begin{pmatrix}0.3&0.1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}d_{12}\\d_{22}\end{pmatrix}=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad d_{12}=-\frac13d_{22}.\]

Choose \(d_{22}=1\). Putting the eigenvectors in columns gives

\[D=\begin{pmatrix}1&-\frac13\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad D^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&\frac13\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\]
\[\Lambda=\begin{pmatrix}0.5&0\\0&0.2\end{pmatrix},\qquad\Phi=D\Lambda D^{-1}.\]

2. Compute the VMA coefficient matrices

In successive powers, \(D^{-1}D=I_2\), so only the diagonal matrix is raised to the power \(j\):

\[\Phi^j=D\Lambda^jD^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&-\frac13\\0&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0.5^j&0\\0&0.2^j\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&\frac13\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.\]
\[\boxed{\Phi^j=\begin{pmatrix}0.5^j&\frac13(0.5^j-0.2^j)\\0&0.2^j\end{pmatrix},\qquad j\ge0.}\]

Thus the VMA(∞) representation is

\[\boxed{Y_t=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\begin{pmatrix}0.5^j&\frac13(0.5^j-0.2^j)\\0&0.2^j\end{pmatrix}\varepsilon_{t-j}.}\]

Componentwise,

\[\begin{aligned}y_{1,t}&=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\left[0.5^j\varepsilon_{1,t-j}+\frac13(0.5^j-0.2^j)\varepsilon_{2,t-j}\right],\\y_{2,t}&=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}0.2^j\varepsilon_{2,t-j}.\end{aligned}\]

Question 2Reduced-form IRFs and cumulative responses

Formulas to use — Question 2

Read the VMA coefficients: the response to a unit innovation at horizon \(h\) is the corresponding coefficient matrix.

\[\Psi_h=\Phi^h,\qquad h\ge0.\]

Add all horizons, including \(h=0\):

\[\sum_{h=0}^{\infty}\Psi_h=(I_2-\Phi)^{-1}.\]

Alternatively, sum each matrix entry using \(\sum_{h=0}^{\infty}r^h=1/(1-r)\), \(|r|<1\).

Solution

Use the matrix powers obtained in Question 1:

\[\boxed{\Psi_h=\Phi^h=\begin{pmatrix}0.5^h&\frac13(0.5^h-0.2^h)\\0&0.2^h\end{pmatrix},\quad h\ge0.}\]

Entry \((i,k)\) gives the response of variable \(i\) to a unit innovation in component \(k\), with the other innovation held at zero. In particular, \(\Psi_0=I_2\) and \(\Psi_1=\Phi\).

Sum the entries over all horizons:

\[\sum_{h=0}^{\infty}\Psi_h=\begin{pmatrix}\frac1{1-0.5}&\frac13\left(\frac1{1-0.5}-\frac1{1-0.2}\right)\\0&\frac1{1-0.2}\end{pmatrix}.\]
\[\boxed{\sum_{h=0}^{\infty}\Psi_h=\begin{pmatrix}2&\frac14\\0&\frac54\end{pmatrix}=(I_2-\Phi)^{-1}.}\]

The reduced-form innovations are correlated because \(\sigma_{12}=0.2\). To describe orthogonal shocks, we next choose an impact matrix.

Question 3Orthogonalised IRFs and shock normalization

Formulas to use — Question 3

Cholesky factorization: choose a lower-triangular impact matrix \(S\) with positive diagonal.

\[SS'=\Sigma_\varepsilon,\qquad\varepsilon_t=Se_t,\qquad V(e_t)=I_2.\]

Propagate the impact matrix: multiply the VMA coefficients on the right by \(S\).

\[\Theta_h=\Phi^hS,\qquad\sum_{h=0}^{\infty}\Theta_h=(I_2-\Phi)^{-1}S.\]

Unit-diagonal normalization: with \(H=\operatorname{diag}(s_{11},s_{22})\), use \(B=SH^{-1}\), \(v_t=He_t\) and \(\widetilde\Theta_h=\Phi^hB\). State which shock has unit size.

Solution

1. Cholesky impact matrix

Use the ordering \((y_1,y_2)\) and unit-variance shocks \(e_t\):

\[\varepsilon_t=Se_t,\qquad V(e_t)=I_2,\qquad S=\begin{pmatrix}s_{11}&0\\s_{21}&s_{22}\end{pmatrix}.\]

Matching \(SS'=\Sigma_\varepsilon\) gives

\[\begin{pmatrix}s_{11}^2&s_{11}s_{21}\\s_{11}s_{21}&s_{21}^2+s_{22}^2\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0.3&0.2\\0.2&0.5\end{pmatrix}.\]
\[\begin{aligned}s_{11}&=\sqrt{0.3},\\s_{21}&=\frac{0.2}{\sqrt{0.3}},\\s_{22}&=\sqrt{0.5-\frac{0.2^2}{0.3}}=\sqrt{\frac{11}{30}}.\end{aligned}\]
\[\boxed{S\simeq\begin{pmatrix}0.547723&0\\0.365148&0.605530\end{pmatrix}.}\]

The zero in the first row means the second orthogonal shock has no contemporaneous effect on \(y_1\).

2. Unit-diagonal normalization

Rescale the shocks using

\[H=\operatorname{diag}\!\left(\sqrt{0.3},\sqrt{\frac{11}{30}}\right),\qquad v_t=He_t.\]

Then \(\varepsilon_t=SH^{-1}v_t=Bv_t\), with

\[B=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\\frac23&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad\Omega=V(v_t)=\begin{pmatrix}0.3&0\\0&\frac{11}{30}\end{pmatrix}.\]

The shocks remain uncorrelated, but their variances are no longer 1. This gives the same innovation covariance:

\[B\Omega B'=\begin{pmatrix}0.3&0.2\\0.2&\frac49(0.3)+\frac{11}{30}\end{pmatrix}=\Sigma_\varepsilon.\]

3. Responses to unit shocks in \(v_t\)

Multiply the coefficient matrix from Question 1 by \(B\):

\[\widetilde\Theta_h=\Phi^hB=\begin{pmatrix}0.5^h&\frac13(0.5^h-0.2^h)\\0&0.2^h\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\\frac23&1\end{pmatrix}.\]
\[\boxed{\widetilde\Theta_h=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{11}{9}0.5^h-\frac29 0.2^h&\frac13(0.5^h-0.2^h)\\\frac23 0.2^h&0.2^h\end{pmatrix},\quad h\ge0.}\]

Each column gives the responses to a unit shock in the corresponding component of \(v_t\). At impact, \(\widetilde\Theta_0=B\). For the long-run cumulative responses, use the sum from Question 2:

\[\sum_{h=0}^{\infty}\widetilde\Theta_h=(I_2-\Phi)^{-1}B=\begin{pmatrix}2&\frac14\\0&\frac54\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\\frac23&1\end{pmatrix}.\]
\[\boxed{\sum_{h=0}^{\infty}\widetilde\Theta_h=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{13}{6}&\frac14\\\frac56&\frac54\end{pmatrix}.}\]

4. Responses to one-standard-deviation shocks

A unit shock in \(e_t\) is a one-standard-deviation shock. Since \(S=BH\), multiply each column above by the corresponding diagonal entry of \(H\):

\[\boxed{\Theta_h=\Phi^hS=\widetilde\Theta_hH.}\]
\[\boxed{\sum_{h=0}^{\infty}\Theta_h=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{13}{6}&\frac14\\\frac56&\frac54\end{pmatrix}H.}\]

Thus \(\widetilde\Theta_h\) uses unit shocks in \(v_t\), while \(\Theta_h\) uses unit-variance shocks \(e_t\). The difference is the scale of each shock.
