LECTURE 7

SVARs: Long-run, Non-recursive Zero and Sign Restrictions

Theory

Long-run restrictions

Consider a stable VAR(1) with \(\varepsilon_t=S\epsilon_t\), \(V(\epsilon_t)=I_n\), and \(\Sigma_\varepsilon=SS'\). Short-run restrictions concern impact; long-run restrictions concern accumulated effects.

Growth and level responses

\[g_t=\Delta y_t=y_t-y_{t-1}\]
\[y_{t+h}=y_{t-1}+\sum_{k=0}^{h}g_{t+k}.\]

Holding the pre-shock history fixed, differentiate with respect to the structural shock:

\[\operatorname{IRF}(h)\big|_{\epsilon_j}^{y}=\sum_{k=0}^{h}\operatorname{IRF}(k)\big|_{\epsilon_j}^{g}.\]

A temporary growth response can leave a permanent level effect because the growth responses accumulate. A zero long-run level effect requires

\[\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\operatorname{IRF}(k)\big|_{\epsilon_j}^{g}=0.\]

Long-run multiplier

\[Y_t=\boldsymbol m+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\Phi^kS\epsilon_{t-k}\]
\[\Theta_h=\Phi^hS.\]

Stability makes the matrix geometric series converge:

\[\begin{aligned}\mathcal C=\operatorname{CIRF}(\infty)&=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\Phi^kS\\&=(I_n-\Phi)^{-1}S=\Psi(1)S,\\\Psi(1)&=(I_n-\Phi)^{-1}.\end{aligned}\]

\(S\) is the impact matrix; \(\Theta_h\) is the response at horizon \(h\); \(\mathcal C\) is the cumulative long-run multiplier. Although \(\Theta_h\to0\), its sum \(\mathcal C\) need not vanish.

Long-run identification

Short-run Cholesky identification makes S triangular. Long-run identification instead makes the cumulative multiplier triangular, leaving impact responses unrestricted:

\[S_{SR}=\begin{pmatrix}\star&0&\cdots&0\\\star&\star&\cdots&0\\\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\\star&\star&\cdots&\star\end{pmatrix}\]
\[\mathcal C=\begin{pmatrix}\star&0&\cdots&0\\\star&\star&\cdots&0\\\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\\star&\star&\cdots&\star\end{pmatrix}.\]
\[\begin{aligned}\mathcal C\mathcal C'&=\Psi(1)SS'\Psi(1)'\\&=\Psi(1)\Sigma_\varepsilon\Psi(1)'\equiv\Lambda.\end{aligned}\]

Obtain \(\Phi,\Sigma_\varepsilon\) from the reduced form; compute \(\Psi(1)\), then \(\Lambda\). Cholesky-factorize \(\Lambda\) with positive diagonal to obtain \(\mathcal C\). Recover the impact matrix:

\[S_{LR}=\Psi(1)^{-1}\mathcal C=(I_n-\Phi)\mathcal C.\]

In general, \(\Lambda\ne V(Y_t)\). Short-run and long-run identification can fit the same reduced form but imply different structural responses:

\[\Xi_h=\Phi^hS_{LR}\]
\[\operatorname{CIRF}(h)=\sum_{k=0}^{h}\Xi_k.\]
Theory

Combining restrictions

Restrictions may concern \(S\), \(\mathcal C\), or both, linked by \(\mathcal C=\Psi(1)S\). With standardized shocks, the number of additional independent identifying restrictions is \(n(n-1)/2\).

Order
Sufficient number of restrictions.
Compatibility
At least one solution satisfies the restrictions and covariance equation.
Rank
Locally independent restrictions rule out nearby equivalent solutions.
Global uniqueness
No distinct admissible solution remains, apart from conventional shock signs.

Too few independent restrictions imply underidentification. The required count gives candidate exact identification; additional restrictions give overidentification. The order condition is necessary, not sufficient. A redundant restriction adds no identifying information. Local identification does not rule out a separate solution elsewhere.

Compatibility in a bivariate system

\[c_{12}=[\Psi(1)]_{11}s_{12}+[\Psi(1)]_{12}s_{22}.\]

If \(s_{12}=0\), nonsingularity requires \(s_{22}\ne0\). Imposing \(c_{12}=0\) as well therefore gives

\[s_{12}=c_{12}=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad[\Psi(1)]_{12}=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\phi_{12}=0.\]

Thus the two restrictions can be incompatible with the estimated dynamics. Counting them is not enough to establish existence or uniqueness.

Theory

Non-recursive zero restrictions

Orthogonal rotations

A triangular ordering may be too restrictive. Start with \(S=\operatorname{chol}(\Sigma_\varepsilon)\) and rotate its columns. Here \(Q\) is an orthogonal rotation, rather than the covariance-standardizing transformation used earlier.

\[Q(\theta)=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta&-\sin\theta\\\sin\theta&\cos\theta\end{pmatrix}\]
\[QQ'=I_n,\quad Q^{-1}=Q'.\]
\[\mathcal P(\theta)=SQ(\theta),\qquad\begin{aligned}\mathcal P(\theta)\mathcal P(\theta)'&=SQ(\theta)Q(\theta)' S'\\&=SS'=\Sigma_\varepsilon.\end{aligned}\]

Rotations preserve the covariance but change which combinations of innovations are interpreted as structural shocks.

\[\Upsilon_{h,\theta}=\Phi^h\mathcal P(\theta)\]
\[\mathcal C(\theta)=\Psi(1)\mathcal P(\theta).\]

Givens rotations

In three dimensions, rotate in each coordinate plane:

\[G(\theta_a)=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta_a&-\sin\theta_a&0\\\sin\theta_a&\cos\theta_a&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix}.\]
\[G(\theta_b)=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta_b&0&-\sin\theta_b\\0&1&0\\\sin\theta_b&0&\cos\theta_b\end{pmatrix}.\]
\[G(\theta_c)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\0&\cos\theta_c&-\sin\theta_c\\0&\sin\theta_c&\cos\theta_c\end{pmatrix}\]
\[Q(\theta)=G(\theta_a)G(\theta_b)G(\theta_c).\]

Imposing non-triangular zeros

\[\mathcal P=\begin{pmatrix}\star&0&\star\\\star&\star&0\\0&\star&\star\end{pmatrix}\]
\[p_{ij}=0\iff\operatorname{IRF}(0)\big|_{\epsilon_j}^{y_i}=0.\]

With \(BY_t=\cdots+\epsilon_t\) and unit-variance shocks, \(\mathcal P=B^{-1}\). A zero in \(B\) is generally not equivalent to a zero in \(B^{-1}\).

Compute the Cholesky factor, construct \(Q(\theta)\), and impose the required entries of \(SQ(\theta)\) equal to zero. Solve the nonlinear equations for the angles, then verify covariance preservation, compatibility, rank and global uniqueness. One Cholesky decomposition generally cannot deliver a non-recursive pattern.

Theory

Sign restrictions

Zero restrictions are equalities. Sign restrictions are inequalities on selected responses, allowing their magnitudes to vary:

\[\operatorname{IRF}(h)\big|_{\epsilon_j}^{y_i}\ge0\]
\[\text{or}\]
\[\operatorname{IRF}(h)\big|_{\epsilon_j}^{y_i}\le0.\]

Let \(d_{ijh}\in\{-1,+1\}\) specify the required sign. The admissible orthogonal matrices satisfy

\[\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{adm}}=\{Q\in O(n):d_{ijh}[\Phi^hSQ]_{ij}\ge0\},\]

for every restricted \((i,j,h)\). Restrictions can apply at impact or over several horizons. Impact signs alone do not determine later signs. Shock signs and labels must be normalized consistently.

Several rotations may satisfy the same inequalities. Sign restrictions therefore usually deliver set identification, rather than one point-identified model.

Monte Carlo rejection sampling

  1. Compute \(S=\operatorname{chol}(\Sigma_\varepsilon)\).
  2. Draw an orthogonal matrix \(Q\) and form \(\mathcal P=SQ\).
  3. Calculate \(\Phi^h\mathcal P\) at the restricted horizons.
  4. Keep the candidate only if all restrictions hold; otherwise discard it.
  5. Repeat to characterize the admissible set.

Uniform orthogonal draws use Haar measure and can be generated through the QR decomposition of a Gaussian random matrix. Exact zeros have zero probability under unrestricted continuous draws: impose the equalities explicitly before applying sign tests.

Interpretation and limitations

The minimum and maximum admissible responses at each horizon form a pointwise identified envelope, not a sampling confidence interval. Identification uncertainty remains even with a fixed reduced form; estimation uncertainty requires separate bootstrap or Bayesian simulation.

Short-run zeros, long-run zeros and signs can be combined. The restrictions require economic justification, and conclusions depend on the horizon, sign conventions and numerical implementation. Selecting one admissible rotation does not establish uniqueness.

Theory

Structural outputs after identification

For each admissible impact matrix and its corresponding unit-variance shocks:

\[\epsilon_t=\mathcal P(\theta)^{-1}\varepsilon_t.\]
\[\operatorname{IRF}(h,\theta)=\Upsilon_{h,\theta}=\Phi^h\mathcal P(\theta)\]
\[\operatorname{CIRF}(h,\theta)=\sum_{k=0}^{h}\Upsilon_{k,\theta}.\]
\[\operatorname{FEVD}(h,\theta)\big|_{\epsilon_j}^{y_i}=\frac{\sum_{k=0}^{h-1}[\Upsilon_{k,\theta}]_{ij}^2}{\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{h-1}[\Upsilon_{k,\theta}]_{i\ell}^2}.\]

For a VAR without deterministic trend, historical decomposition retains the initial-condition term:

\[\begin{aligned}Y_t-\boldsymbol m={}&\Phi^t(Y_0-\boldsymbol m)\\&+\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\Phi^k\mathcal P(\theta)\epsilon_{t-k}.\end{aligned}\]

IRFs, FEVD and historical contributions can differ across admissible impact matrices even when the reduced-form dynamics and covariance are unchanged.
