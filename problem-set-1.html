PROBLEM SET 1

Exercise 1

An AR(1) process

Consider the process

\[X_t=0.75X_{t-1}+\varepsilon_t,\]

where \(\varepsilon_t\) is a weak white noise.

  1. Write the autoregressive lag polynomial.
  2. Determine the characteristic equation, the inverse characteristic equation, and their roots.
  3. Find the inverse lag polynomial and the MA\((\infty)\) representation.
  4. Compute the ACF and PACF up to \(h=2\).

Given information & notation

For the weak white noise,

\[E(\varepsilon_t)=0,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon_t)=\sigma_\varepsilon^2.\] \[\operatorname{Cov}(\varepsilon_t,\varepsilon_{t-h})=0\quad(h\ne0).\]

The lag operator is \(LX_t=X_{t-1}\). Below, we use the stable, causal solution of the process.

Step 1 Autoregressive lag polynomial

Use \(L\) to move the lagged term to the left:

\[X_t-0.75LX_t=\varepsilon_t\] \[(1-0.75L)X_t=\varepsilon_t.\]
\[\boxed{\Phi(L)=1-0.75L}\]

Step 2 Characteristic equations & roots

Characteristic equation

Set the lag polynomial equal to zero:

\[\Phi(z)=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad 1-0.75z=0\] \[0.75z=1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad z=\frac{1}{0.75}=\frac43.\]

Inverse characteristic equation

For an AR(1), multiply \(\Phi(r^{-1})\) by \(r\):

\[r\Phi(r^{-1})=0\] \[r(1-0.75r^{-1})=0\] \[r-0.75=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad r=0.75.\]
\[z=\frac43>1,\qquad r=\frac1z=0.75<1.\]

The characteristic root lies outside the unit circle; its inverse lies inside. The stability condition is satisfied.

Step 3 Inverse polynomial & MA\((\infty)\)

Since \(|0.75|<1\), expand the inverse using the geometric series:

\[\Phi(L)^{-1}=(1-0.75L)^{-1}\] \[\boxed{\Phi(L)^{-1}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}0.75^jL^j}.\]

Apply this inverse to \(\varepsilon_t\), with \(L^j\varepsilon_t=\varepsilon_{t-j}\):

\[X_t=\Phi(L)^{-1}\varepsilon_t\] \[X_t=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}0.75^jL^j\varepsilon_t.\]
\[\boxed{X_t=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}0.75^j\varepsilon_{t-j}}\]
\(X_t=\varepsilon_t+0.75\varepsilon_{t-1}\)\({}+0.75^2\varepsilon_{t-2}+\cdots.\)

Step 4 ACF & PACF up to \(h=2\)

The causal representation has zero mean. Write

\[\gamma_X(h)=\operatorname{Cov}(X_t,X_{t-h})\]
\(\gamma_X(h)=E(X_tX_{t-h}),\)\(\rho_X(h)=\frac{\gamma_X(h)}{\gamma_X(0)}.\)

ACF — Method 1: MA\((\infty)\)

Multiply the two MA representations and take expectations, for \(h\ge0\):

\[X_{t-h}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}0.75^k\varepsilon_{t-h-k}\] \[\gamma_X(h)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}0.75^{j+k}\,E(\varepsilon_{t-j}\varepsilon_{t-h-k}).\]

White-noise terms contribute only when their dates coincide:

\[t-j=t-h-k\quad\Longrightarrow\quad k=j-h.\] \[k\ge0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad j\ge h.\]

For matching dates, the expectation is \(\sigma_\varepsilon^2\). Thus

\[\gamma_X(h)=\sigma_\varepsilon^2\sum_{j=h}^{\infty}0.75^{2j-h}\] \[=\sigma_\varepsilon^2\,0.75^{-h}\sum_{j=h}^{\infty}0.75^{2j}\] \[=\sigma_\varepsilon^2\,0.75^{-h}\frac{0.75^{2h}}{1-0.75^2}\] \[=\frac{\sigma_\varepsilon^2\,0.75^h}{1-0.75^2}.\]

Attention. The sum starts at \(j=h\), not \(j=0\), because \(k=j-h\ge0\). This is why the final power is \(0.75^h\).

\[\gamma_X(0)=\frac{\sigma_\varepsilon^2}{1-0.75^2}\] \[\rho_X(h)=\frac{\gamma_X(h)}{\gamma_X(0)}=0.75^h\quad(h\ge0).\]

ACF — Method 2: Yule–Walker / AR recursion

Multiply the AR equation by \(X_{t-h}\) and take expectations:

\(E(X_tX_{t-h})=0.75E(X_{t-1}X_{t-h})\)\({}+E(\varepsilon_tX_{t-h}).\)

For \(h>0\), \(X_{t-h}\) contains only past innovations, which are uncorrelated with \(\varepsilon_t\). Hence the last term is zero:

\[\gamma_X(h)=0.75\gamma_X(h-1)\] \[\rho_X(h)=0.75\rho_X(h-1).\]

Starting from \(\rho_X(0)=1\),

\[\rho_X(1)=0.75,\] \[\rho_X(2)=0.75\times0.75=0.5625.\]

PACF

Write \(\alpha_X(h)\) for the correlation after controlling for the intermediate lags. At lag 1, there is no intermediate lag:

\[\alpha_X(1)=\rho_X(1)=0.75.\]

At lag 2, control for \(X_{t-1}\):

\[\alpha_X(2)=\frac{\rho_X(2)-[\rho_X(1)]^2}{1-[\rho_X(1)]^2}\] \[=\frac{0.5625-0.75^2}{1-0.75^2}=\frac{0}{0.4375}=0.\]
