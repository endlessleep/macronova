LECTURE 3

ARMA Processes and Their Properties

Part 1

Class of ARMA Processes

Definition of an ARMA(p,q) process

An ARMA\((p,q)\) combines past observations and shocks:

\[\begin{aligned}X_t={}&\mu+\phi_1X_{t-1}+\cdots+\phi_pX_{t-p}\\&+\varepsilon_t+\theta_1\varepsilon_{t-1}+\cdots+\theta_q\varepsilon_{t-q}.\end{aligned}\]

AR part

\(p\) lags of \(X_t\); \(\phi_i\) multiplies \(X_{t-i}\). For \(p>0\), \(\phi_p\ne0\).

MA part

\(q\) lagged shocks; \(\theta_j\) multiplies \(\varepsilon_{t-j}\). For \(q>0\), \(\theta_q\ne0\).

Assume weak white noise with positive variance:

\[E(\varepsilon_t)=0,\qquad 0<\operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon_t)=\sigma_\varepsilon^2<\infty,\]\[E(\varepsilon_t\varepsilon_s)=0\quad(t\ne s).\]

\(\mu\) is the intercept, generally different from \(E(X_t)\). The earlier zero-mean models use \(\mu=0\).

Lag-polynomial representation

\[L^iX_t=X_{t-i}.\]

Move the AR terms to the left; keep the shocks on the right:

\[\Phi(L)=1-\phi_1L-\cdots-\phi_pL^p,\]\[\Theta(L)=1+\theta_1L+\cdots+\theta_qL^q.\]

AR: minus signs. MA: original signs. Each coefficient may itself be positive or negative.

\[\boxed{\Phi(L)X_t=\mu+\Theta(L)\varepsilon_t.}\]

Subtract the AR terms:

\[\begin{aligned}X_t-\sum_{i=1}^p\phi_iX_{t-i}&=\mu+\varepsilon_t+\sum_{j=1}^q\theta_j\varepsilon_{t-j}\\\left(1-\sum_{i=1}^p\phi_iL^i\right)X_t&=\mu+\left(1+\sum_{j=1}^q\theta_jL^j\right)\varepsilon_t.\end{aligned}\]

The brackets define \(\Phi(L)\) and \(\Theta(L)\).

AR(p) and MA(q) as special cases

Autoregressive process: AR(p)

\(q=0\), \(\Theta(L)=1\), and \(\phi_p\ne0\):

\[X_t=\mu+\phi_1X_{t-1}+\cdots+\phi_pX_{t-p}+\varepsilon_t.\]
\[\boxed{\Phi(L)X_t=\mu+\varepsilon_t.}\]

Moving-average process: MA(q)

\(p=0\), \(\Phi(L)=1\), and \(\theta_q\ne0\):

\[X_t=\mu+\varepsilon_t+\theta_1\varepsilon_{t-1}+\cdots+\theta_q\varepsilon_{t-q}.\]
\[\boxed{X_t=\mu+\Theta(L)\varepsilon_t.}\]

The current-shock coefficient is 1. MA weights need not sum to 1.

The intercept and the mean

For a stationary solution, \(E(X_t)=m_X\) and \(\Phi(1)\ne0\). Take expectations:

\[\begin{aligned}\underbrace{E(X_t)}_{m_X}={}&\mu+\sum_{i=1}^{p}\phi_i\underbrace{E(X_{t-i})}_{m_X}\\&+\underbrace{E[\Theta(L)\varepsilon_t]}_{0}.\end{aligned}\]
\[\boxed{m_X=\frac{\mu}{1-\sum_{i=1}^p\phi_i}=\frac{\mu}{\Phi(1)}.}\]

\(\Phi(1)=1-\sum_{i=1}^p\phi_i\). For an MA, \(\Phi(1)=1\), so \(m_X=\mu\).

1. Take expectations term by term

\[E(X_t)=\mu+\sum_{i=1}^p\phi_iE(X_{t-i})+E(\varepsilon_t)+\sum_{j=1}^q\theta_jE(\varepsilon_{t-j}).\]

Stationarity: \(E(X_{t-i})=m_X\). White noise: \(E(\varepsilon_{t-j})=0\).

\[m_X=\mu+\sum_{i=1}^p\phi_i m_X.\]

2. Collect the terms in the mean

\[m_X-\left(\sum_{i=1}^p\phi_i\right)m_X=\mu,\]\[\left(1-\sum_{i=1}^p\phi_i\right)m_X=\mu,\]\[\Phi(1)m_X=\mu.\]

3. Divide by the nonzero coefficient

No root at \(z=1\) means \(\Phi(1)\ne0\), so division is valid:

\[m_X=\frac{\mu}{\Phi(1)}.\]

\(\mu=0\Rightarrow m_X=0\).

Mean-adjusted ARMA

Subtract the mean:

\[\widetilde X_t=X_t-m_X,\qquad E(\widetilde X_t)=m_X-m_X=0.\]
\[\boxed{\widetilde X_t=X_t-\frac{\mu}{1-\sum_{i=1}^p\phi_i}=X_t-\frac{\mu}{\Phi(1)}.}\]\[\boxed{\Phi(L)\widetilde X_t=\Theta(L)\varepsilon_t.}\]

Centring removes the intercept; the AR/MA coefficients, covariances and correlations stay the same.

Substitute \(X_t=\widetilde X_t+m_X\):

\[\widetilde X_t+m_X=\mu+\sum_{i=1}^p\phi_i(\widetilde X_{t-i}+m_X)+\Theta(L)\varepsilon_t.\]\[\widetilde X_t=\sum_{i=1}^p\phi_i\widetilde X_{t-i}+\Theta(L)\varepsilon_t+\left[\mu+\sum_{i=1}^p\phi_i m_X-m_X\right].\]

Use \(\mu=\Phi(1)m_X\):

\[\mu+\sum_{i=1}^p\phi_i m_X-m_X=\mu-\Phi(1)m_X=0.\]\[\widetilde X_t-\sum_{i=1}^p\phi_i\widetilde X_{t-i}=\Theta(L)\varepsilon_t.\]\[\Phi(L)\widetilde X_t=\Theta(L)\varepsilon_t.\]

Fixed constants do not affect covariance:

\[\operatorname{Cov}(X_t-m_X,X_s-m_X)=\operatorname{Cov}(X_t,X_s).\]
Part 2

Stationarity, Stability and Invertibility Conditions

Stationarity

Does a stationary solution exist?

Stability / causality

Can observations be built from current and past shocks?

Causal invertibility

Can shocks be recovered from current and past observations?

Unit circle and root conventions

\[z=a+ib,\qquad |z|=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}.\]

The unit circle contains points at distance 1 from zero:

\[\mathcal C=\{z\in\mathbb C:\ |z|=1\}.\]

Assumption: \(\Phi\) and \(\Theta\) have no common roots, so no AR/MA factor cancels.

A root makes its polynomial zero. Substitute \(z=1/r\) and multiply by \(r^p\):

\[\Phi(z)=1-\phi_1z-\cdots-\phi_pz^p=0,\]\[r^p\Phi(1/r)=r^p-\phi_1r^{p-1}-\cdots-\phi_p=0.\]
\[r_i=\frac1{z_i},\qquad |r_i|=\frac1{|z_i|}.\]

Outside roots become inside inverse roots; unit-circle roots stay on the circle.

\(\Phi(0)=1\Rightarrow z_i\ne0\). Set \(r_i=1/z_i\):

\[r_i^p\Phi(1/r_i)=r_i^p\Phi(z_i)=0.\]

Conversely, \(\phi_p\ne0\Rightarrow r_i\ne0\). Divide by \(r_i^p\):

\[r_i^p\Phi(1/r_i)=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\Phi(1/r_i)=0.\]
\[|r_i|=\frac1{|z_i|},\]\[|z_i|>1\iff|r_i|<1,\qquad |z_i|\ne1\iff|r_i|\ne1.\]

For the MA polynomial, use roots \(w_i\) and inverse roots \(s_i\):

\[\Theta(w_i)=0,\qquad s_i^q\Theta(1/s_i)=0,\qquad s_i=1/w_i.\]

Stationarity: existence and uniqueness

Stationarity: no AR root lies on the unit circle. Equivalently,

\[\boxed{\Phi(z_i)=0\implies |z_i|\ne1\quad\text{for every }i.}\]\[\boxed{r_i^p\Phi(1/r_i)=0\implies |r_i|\ne1\quad\text{for every }i.}\]

Inside and outside roots are allowed; roots on the circle are excluded.

Theorem — unique stationary solution

Under weak white noise, no common roots, and \(|z_i|\ne1\) for every AR root, the unique stationary solution is

\[\boxed{X_t=m_X+\Psi(L)\varepsilon_t,}\]\[\Psi(L)=\frac{\Theta(L)}{\Phi(L)},\qquad m_X=\frac{\mu}{\Phi(1)}.\]

\(\Phi(L)^{-1}\) is an inverse filter. A bilateral Laurent series allows both lags and leads:

\[\Psi(L)=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\psi_jL^j,\]\[X_t=m_X+\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\psi_j\varepsilon_{t-j}.\]
\[j>0:\ \varepsilon_{t-j}\text{ (past)},\qquad j=0:\ \varepsilon_t,\]\[j<0:\ \varepsilon_{t-j}\text{ (future)},\qquad\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}|\psi_j|<\infty.\]

The weights are absolutely summable; truncation errors tend to zero in mean square.

Boundary in the source statement

The source states:

\[|z_i|\le1\ \Longrightarrow\ \Psi(L)=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\psi_jL^j.\]

Boundary issue: \(|z_i|=1\) contradicts the theorem. Strictly inside roots require leads; mixed inside/outside roots generally require both leads and lags.

1. Remove the mean and invert the AR polynomial

\[\Phi(L)\widetilde X_t=\Theta(L)\varepsilon_t,\]\[\widetilde X_t=\Phi(L)^{-1}\Theta(L)\varepsilon_t=\Psi(L)\varepsilon_t.\]

Factor \(\Phi\) using \(r_i=1/z_i\):

\[\Phi(L)=\prod_{i=1}^p(1-r_iL).\]

Invert each factor by a convergent geometric series:

\[(1-r_iL)^{-1}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}r_i^kL^k\quad(|r_i|<1),\]\[(1-r_iL)^{-1}=-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}r_i^{-k}L^{-k}\quad(|r_i|>1).\]

Each series is absolutely summable. Finite products, and multiplication by \(\Theta\), preserve this property. The excluded case is \(|r_i|=1\).

2. Check the moments

\[\sum_j|\psi_j|<\infty\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\sum_j\psi_j^2<\infty,\]\[E\!\left[\left(\sum_{|j|>N}\psi_j\varepsilon_{t-j}\right)^2\right]=\sigma_\varepsilon^2\sum_{|j|>N}\psi_j^2\longrightarrow0.\]

White-noise orthogonality gives:

\[E(X_t)=m_X,\]\[\operatorname{Var}(X_t)=\sigma_\varepsilon^2\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}\psi_j^2<\infty.\]\[\begin{aligned}\operatorname{Cov}(X_t,X_{t-h})&=\sum_{j,k\in\mathbb Z}\psi_j\psi_k E(\varepsilon_{t-j}\varepsilon_{t-h-k})\\&=\sigma_\varepsilon^2\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}\psi_j\psi_{j-h}.\end{aligned}\]

Equal dates: \(t-j=t-h-k\iff k=j-h\). Covariance depends only on \(h\): stationarity.

3. Check the equation and uniqueness

\[\begin{aligned}\Phi(L)X_t&=\Phi(1)m_X+\Phi(L)\Psi(L)\varepsilon_t\\&=\mu+\Theta(L)\varepsilon_t.\end{aligned}\]

Applying the same convergent inverse to any stationary solution fixes \(\widetilde X_t=\Psi(L)\varepsilon_t\). The mean equation fixes \(m_X\): uniqueness.

Why every finite MA(q) is stationary

\(\Phi(L)=1\): no AR roots to restrict. With \(\theta_0=1\),

\[E(X_t)=\mu,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(X_t)=\sigma_\varepsilon^2\sum_{j=0}^q\theta_j^2,\qquad\theta_0=1.\]

Finite MA coefficients give finite variance and covariance depending only on the lag. Invertibility is not required for stationarity.

Stability: a causal representation

Stability: every AR root is strictly outside the unit circle:

\[\boxed{|z_i|>1\iff |r_i|<1\quad\text{for every AR root}.}\]

Only non-negative powers of \(L\) occur: a unilateral MA(∞).

\[\Psi(L)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\psi_jL^j.\]
\[\boxed{X_t=m_X+\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\psi_j\varepsilon_{t-j}.}\]

Causal: current and past shocks only. Stability implies stationarity; stationarity alone also allows future shocks.

Multiply a finite geometric sum by \(1-rL\):

\[(1-rL)\sum_{k=0}^{n}r^kL^k=1-r^{n+1}L^{n+1}.\]

For \(|r|<1\), the remainder vanishes in mean square. For white noise:

\[E|r^{n+1}\varepsilon_{t-n-1}|^2=|r|^{2n+2}\sigma_\varepsilon^2\longrightarrow0.\]

Multiply the inverse factors:

\[\Phi(L)^{-1}=\prod_{i=1}^p\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}r_i^kL^k\right),\]\[\Psi(L)=\Theta(L)\Phi(L)^{-1}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\psi_jL^j.\]

Use \(L^j\varepsilon_t=\varepsilon_{t-j}\). To find the weights, match powers in \(\Phi(L)\Psi(L)=\Theta(L)\):

\[\psi_0=1,\qquad \psi_j-\sum_{i=1}^{\min(p,j)}\phi_i\psi_{j-i}=\theta_j\quad(j\ge1),\]

Set \(\theta_j=0\) for \(j>q\).

Invertibility: recovering the shocks

To recover unobserved shocks from observations, isolate \(\Theta(L)\varepsilon_t\) and invert it. Assume \(X_t\) stationary and all MA roots outside the unit circle:

\[\boxed{\Theta(w_i)=0\implies |w_i|>1\quad\text{for every MA root}.}\]\[\boxed{\varepsilon_t=m_X^*+\frac{\Phi(L)}{\Theta(L)}X_t,}\]\[\boxed{m_X^*=-\frac{\mu}{\Theta(1)}=-\frac{\Phi(1)}{\Theta(1)}m_X.}\]

\(m_X^*\) corrects for the observation mean. With causality as well, the recovered shocks are innovations.

1. Start from the ARMA equation

\[\Phi(L)X_t=\mu+\Theta(L)\varepsilon_t.\]

Subtract the intercept:

\[\Theta(L)\varepsilon_t=\Phi(L)X_t-\mu.\]

2. Apply the inverse MA filter

All \(|w_i|>1\): the causal inverse converges. Apply it to both terms:

\[\varepsilon_t=\frac1{\Theta(L)}\Phi(L)X_t-\frac1{\Theta(L)}\mu.\]

3. Work out what the filter does to the constant

Lagging a constant leaves it unchanged:

\[L\mu=\mu,\qquad L^j\mu=\mu.\]\[\begin{aligned}\Theta(L)\mu&=\mu+\theta_1L\mu+\cdots+\theta_qL^q\mu\\&=(1+\theta_1+\cdots+\theta_q)\mu\\&=\Theta(1)\mu.\end{aligned}\]

\(\Theta(1)\ne0\). Check the inverse image of the constant:

\[\Theta(L)\left(\frac{\mu}{\Theta(1)}\right)=\Theta(1)\frac{\mu}{\Theta(1)}=\mu,\]\[\frac1{\Theta(L)}\mu=\frac{\mu}{\Theta(1)}.\]

4. Identify the recovered-shock constant

\[\varepsilon_t=\frac{\Phi(L)}{\Theta(L)}X_t-\frac{\mu}{\Theta(1)}.\]\[m_X^*=-\frac{\mu}{\Theta(1)}.\]

Substitute \(\mu=\Phi(1)m_X\):

\[m_X^*=-\frac{\Phi(1)m_X}{\Theta(1)}=-\frac{\Phi(1)}{\Theta(1)}m_X.\]

The shock is recovered from current and past observations, with the constant correcting their nonzero mean.

Recap of conditions

The source uses “invertibility” below in the broader, two-sided sense:

Stationarity

\[\text{All AR roots: }|z_i|\ne1.\]

A stationary solution; future shocks are allowed.

Stability

\[\text{All AR roots: }|z_i|>1.\]

Current and past shocks only.

Invertibility — wording of the recap

\[\text{All MA roots: }|w_i|\ne1.\]

A two-sided inverse; future observations are allowed.

Two meanings of invertibility
\[|w_i|\ne1:\quad\text{bilateral inverse allowed},\]\[|w_i|>1:\quad\text{causal inverse}.\]

Inside MA roots require leads. The recap’s weaker condition does not guarantee recovery from the present and past only.

Part 3

Representations

Fundamental representation

Fundamental: shocks are recoverable from present and past observations. This requires a stationary solution and a causal MA inverse.

AR condition in the supplied definition

The supplied definition states, for every respective root:

\[\text{AR roots: }|z_i|\le1\quad\text{(labelled “stationarity only”)},\]\[\text{MA roots: }|w_i|>1\quad\text{(invertibility)}.\]

AR condition issue: \(|z_i|\le1\) includes forbidden unit roots and excludes stable roots. It conflicts with the theorem and the causal AR(1) case.

Use the distinction:

\[\underbrace{|z_i|\ne1}_{\text{stationarity}},\qquad\underbrace{|w_i|>1}_{\text{shock recovery}}.\]

Causality is a separate AR condition.

Recoverable shocks are not necessarily prediction errors: identifying innovations also requires causality.

Causal and fundamental representation

Causal and fundamental: both filters use the present and past only:

\[\boxed{\begin{aligned}\Phi(z_i)=0&\implies |z_i|>1\quad\text{for every AR root},\\\Theta(w_i)=0&\implies |w_i|>1\quad\text{for every MA root}.\end{aligned}}\]
\[\text{AR roots outside}\ \Longrightarrow\ X_t\text{ from current/past shocks},\]\[\text{MA roots outside}\ \Longrightarrow\ \varepsilon_t\text{ from current/past observations}.\]

Proposition: innovations and infinite representations

For a causal and fundamental representation, \(\varepsilon_t\) is the innovation: the one-step linear prediction error.

There exist real coefficient sequences \((a_k)_{k\ge0}\) and \((b_k)_{k\ge0}\) such that:

MA(∞): observations as weighted shocks

\[\boxed{X_t=m_X+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_k\varepsilon_{t-k}.}\]

\(a_k\): weight of the shock \(k\) periods ago.

\[\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_kL^k=\frac{\Theta(L)}{\Phi(L)},\qquad a_k=\psi_k,\quad a_0=1.\]

AR(∞): shocks recovered from observations

\[\boxed{\varepsilon_t=m_X^*+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}b_kX_{t-k}.}\]

\(b_k\): weight of the observation \(k\) periods ago.

\[\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}b_kL^k=\frac{\Phi(L)}{\Theta(L)},\qquad b_0=1.\]

Isolate the current observation:

\[X_t=-m_X^*-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}b_kX_{t-k}+\varepsilon_t.\]

The corresponding AR coefficients are \(-b_k\).

1. Expand each valid inverse filter

\[\frac{\Theta(L)}{\Phi(L)}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_kL^k,\qquad \frac{\Phi(L)}{\Theta(L)}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}b_kL^k.\]

Both filters converge under the root conditions. Since \(\Phi(0)=\Theta(0)=1\), matching \(L^0\) gives \(a_0=b_0=1\).

2. Separate the predictable part

\[X_t=m_X+\varepsilon_t+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_k\varepsilon_{t-k}.\]

Invertibility: past shocks are recoverable from past observations, so the sum is predictable.

Causality + white noise: \(\varepsilon_t\) is orthogonal to past observations. Hence

\[\varepsilon_t=X_t-\widehat X_{t\mid t-1},\qquad \widehat X_{t\mid t-1}=m_X+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_k\varepsilon_{t-k}.\]

\(\widehat X_{t\mid t-1}\): best linear predictor from all past observations.

3. Recover the AR form and its intercept

\[\varepsilon_t=m_X^*+b_0X_t+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}b_kX_{t-k},\]\[X_t=-m_X^*-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}b_kX_{t-k}+\varepsilon_t.\]

Check the mean of the recovered shock:

\[\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}b_k=\frac{\Phi(1)}{\Theta(1)},\]\[E(\varepsilon_t)=m_X^*+m_X\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}b_k=-\frac{\Phi(1)}{\Theta(1)}m_X+\frac{\Phi(1)}{\Theta(1)}m_X=0.\]

Example: AR(1)

Take the zero-intercept model with Gaussian white noise:

\[(1-\rho L)X_t=\varepsilon_t,\qquad \varepsilon_t\overset{iid}{\sim}\mathcal N(0,\sigma_\varepsilon^2).\]

For \(\rho\ne0\), solve the characteristic equation:

\[\Phi(z)=1-\rho z=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad z_1=\frac1\rho.\]
\[\text{Stationarity: }|z_1|\ne1\iff|\rho|\ne1,\]\[\text{Stability: }|z_1|>1\iff|\rho|<1.\]

\(\rho=0\Rightarrow X_t=\varepsilon_t\): stationary and causal, with no AR root.

Case 1: |ρ| < 1 — stationary and causal

\(|\rho|<1\): geometric weights decay. Also \(\Theta(L)=1\), so the representation is causal and fundamental.

\[\Phi(L)^{-1}=\frac1{1-\rho L}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\rho^kL^k,\]\[\boxed{X_t=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\rho^k\varepsilon_{t-k}.}\]

Substitute \(X_{t-1}=\rho X_{t-2}+\varepsilon_{t-1}\):

\[\begin{aligned}X_t&=\rho(\rho X_{t-2}+\varepsilon_{t-1})+\varepsilon_t\\&=\varepsilon_t+\rho\varepsilon_{t-1}+\rho^2X_{t-2}.\end{aligned}\]

After \(n+1\) substitutions,

\[X_t=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\rho^k\varepsilon_{t-k}+\rho^{n+1}X_{t-n-1}.\]

The stationary remainder vanishes in mean square:

\[E[(\rho^{n+1}X_{t-n-1})^2]=\rho^{2n+2}E(X_t^2)\longrightarrow0\qquad(|\rho|<1).\]

Case 2: |ρ| > 1 — stationary and noncausal

Lag weights grow. Use decaying lead weights instead:

\[F=L^{-1},\qquad F^k\varepsilon_t=\varepsilon_{t+k},\qquad |\rho^{-1}|<1.\]
\[\Phi(L)^{-1}=-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\rho^{-k}F^k,\]\[\boxed{X_t=-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\rho^{-k}\varepsilon_{t+k}.}\]

Fundamental but noncausal: shocks remain recoverable, although observations use future shocks.

\[\varepsilon_t=X_t-\rho X_{t-1}.\]

These driving shocks are not the innovations of the causal-and-fundamental proposition.

1. Factor the operator using a lead

Use \(LF=1\):

\[-\rho L(1-\rho^{-1}F)=-\rho L+LF=1-\rho L.\]

2. Invert the factors

\[\frac1{1-\rho L}=-\rho^{-1}F\frac1{1-\rho^{-1}F}.\]

\(|\rho^{-1}|<1\): expand geometrically in \(F\):

\[\begin{aligned}\frac1{1-\rho L}&=-\rho^{-1}F\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\rho^{-j}F^j\\&=-\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\rho^{-(j+1)}F^{j+1}\\&=-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\rho^{-k}F^k.\end{aligned}\]

3. Apply the inverse to the shocks

\[X_t=-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\rho^{-k}\varepsilon_{t+k}.\]
\[\operatorname{Var}(X_t)=\sigma_\varepsilon^2\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\rho^{-2k}=\frac{\sigma_\varepsilon^2}{\rho^2-1}<\infty.\]

Square-summable weights give mean-square convergence.

4. Check that it solves the original equation

\[\begin{aligned}\rho X_{t-1}&=-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\rho^{1-k}\varepsilon_{t-1+k}\\&=-\varepsilon_t-\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\rho^{-j}\varepsilon_{t+j}\\&=-\varepsilon_t+X_t.\end{aligned}\]

Thus \(X_t-\rho X_{t-1}=\varepsilon_t\).

Case 3: |ρ| = 1 — no stationary solution

Unit-circle root; neither lag nor lead weights decay. No stationary finite-variance solution; no stability.

Suppose a stationary finite-variance solution exists. Repeated substitution gives

\[X_t-\rho^{n+1}X_{t-n-1}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\rho^k\varepsilon_{t-k}.\]

For \(|\rho|=1\), uncorrelated shocks give:

\[\operatorname{Var}\!\left(\sum_{k=0}^n\rho^k\varepsilon_{t-k}\right)=(n+1)\sigma_\varepsilon^2\longrightarrow\infty.\]

But stationarity and the covariance bound give:

\[|\gamma_X(h)|\le\gamma_X(0),\]\[\begin{aligned}\operatorname{Var}(X_t-\rho^{n+1}X_{t-n-1})&=2\gamma_X(0)-2\rho^{n+1}\gamma_X(n+1)\\&\le4\gamma_X(0)<\infty.\end{aligned}\]

Contradiction: the same variance cannot diverge and remain bounded.

Example: MA(1)

Take the finite moving average:

\[X_t=\varepsilon_t+\theta\varepsilon_{t-1},\qquad \varepsilon_t\overset{iid}{\sim}\mathcal N(0,\sigma_\varepsilon^2).\]

For \(\theta\ne0\),

\[\Theta(z)=1+\theta z=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad z_1=-\frac1\theta.\]

The MA root controls inversion; finite MA processes are always stationary.

\[\boxed{\text{Stationary for every finite }\theta.}\]\[\boxed{\text{Causally invertible: }|z_1|>1\iff|\theta|<1.}\]

\(|\theta|<1\): a convergent inverse in present and past observations:

\[\varepsilon_t=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-\theta)^kX_{t-k}.\]

\(|\theta|\ge1\): no such causal stable inverse, but stationarity remains. \(\theta=0\): white noise, with no root to check.

Use \(X_t=(1+\theta L)\varepsilon_t\) and the geometric identity:

\[(1+\theta L)\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-\theta)^kL^k=1-(-\theta)^{n+1}L^{n+1}.\]

For stationary finite-variance \(X_t\) and \(|\theta|<1\),

\[E|(-\theta)^{n+1}X_{t-n-1}|^2=|\theta|^{2n+2}E(X_t^2)\longrightarrow0.\]

Therefore:

\[(1+\theta L)^{-1}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-\theta)^kL^k,\]\[\begin{aligned}\varepsilon_t&=(1+\theta L)^{-1}X_t\\&=X_t-\theta X_{t-1}+\theta^2X_{t-2}-\cdots.\end{aligned}\]

Isolate the current observation:

\[X_t=-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(-\theta)^kX_{t-k}+\varepsilon_t.\]

The same process now has an AR(∞) representation.

Part 4

Properties of the Autocorrelation Function

For stationary \(X_t\) with positive variance:

\[\gamma_X(h)=E[(X_t-m_X)(X_{t-h}-m_X)],\]\[\rho_X(h)=\frac{\gamma_X(h)}{\gamma_X(0)},\qquad \rho_X(0)=1.\]

\(\gamma_X(h)\): autocovariance; \(\gamma_X(0)\): variance. The source’s mean notation \(\mu_X=m_X\) differs from the intercept \(\mu\).

ACF of an MA(q) process

For a fundamental MA\((q)\), set \(\theta_0=1\):

\[X_t=\mu+\sum_{j=0}^q\theta_j\varepsilon_{t-j},\qquad \widetilde X_t=\sum_{j=0}^q\theta_j\varepsilon_{t-j}.\]

Match shock dates. Different dates contribute zero:

\[\gamma_X(0)=\sigma_\varepsilon^2\sum_{j=0}^q\theta_j^2,\]\[\gamma_X(h)=\sigma_\varepsilon^2\sum_{j=0}^{q-h}\theta_j\theta_{j+h}\quad(1\le h\le q).\]
\[\boxed{\rho_X(h)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{j=0}^{q-h}\theta_j\theta_{j+h}}{\sum_{j=0}^q\theta_j^2},&1\le h\le q,\\[8pt]0,&h>q.\end{cases}}\]

\(\sigma_\varepsilon^2\) cancels. For \(h>q\), no common shock remains: the ACF cuts off after \(q\).

At \(h=q\), only \(\theta_0\theta_q=\theta_q\) remains. Use symmetry for negative lags:

\[\rho_X(q)=\frac{\theta_q}{\sum_{j=0}^q\theta_j^2},\qquad \rho_X(-h)=\rho_X(h).\]

1. Expand the variance

\[\begin{aligned}\gamma_X(0)&=E\left[\left(\sum_{j=0}^q\theta_j\varepsilon_{t-j}\right)\left(\sum_{k=0}^q\theta_k\varepsilon_{t-k}\right)\right]\\&=\sum_{j=0}^q\sum_{k=0}^q\theta_j\theta_k E(\varepsilon_{t-j}\varepsilon_{t-k}).\end{aligned}\]
\[E(\varepsilon_{t-j}\varepsilon_{t-k})=\begin{cases}\sigma_\varepsilon^2,&j=k,\\0,&j\ne k.\end{cases}\]
\[\gamma_X(0)=\sigma_\varepsilon^2\sum_{j=0}^q\theta_j^2.\]

2. Match dates at a positive lag

\[\widetilde X_{t-h}=\sum_{k=0}^q\theta_k\varepsilon_{t-h-k},\]\[\gamma_X(h)=\sum_{j=0}^q\sum_{k=0}^q\theta_j\theta_k E(\varepsilon_{t-j}\varepsilon_{t-h-k}).\]

The dates must be equal for a term to survive:

\[t-j=t-h-k\iff j=h+k.\]
\[j=h+k\le q\quad\Longrightarrow\quad0\le k\le q-h.\]
\[\gamma_X(h)=\sigma_\varepsilon^2\sum_{k=0}^{q-h}\theta_{h+k}\theta_k.\]

\(h>q\Rightarrow q-h<0\): no surviving pair, so \(\gamma_X(h)=0\).

3. Divide by the variance

\[\rho_X(h)=\frac{\sigma_\varepsilon^2\sum_{k=0}^{q-h}\theta_{h+k}\theta_k}{\sigma_\varepsilon^2\sum_{j=0}^q\theta_j^2}=\frac{\sum_{k=0}^{q-h}\theta_{h+k}\theta_k}{\sum_{j=0}^q\theta_j^2}.\]

At \(h=q\), only \(k=0\) remains. For symmetry, swap observations and shift dates:

\[\begin{aligned}\gamma_X(-h)&=\operatorname{Cov}(X_t,X_{t+h})\\&=\operatorname{Cov}(X_{t+h},X_t)\\&=\operatorname{Cov}(X_t,X_{t-h})=\gamma_X(h).\end{aligned}\]

Divide by \(\gamma_X(0)\): \(\rho_X(-h)=\rho_X(h)\).

ACF of a stationary causal AR(p)

Assume causality: current shocks are orthogonal to past observations. Centre the AR equation:

\[X_t-m_X=\phi_1(X_{t-1}-m_X)+\cdots+\phi_p(X_{t-p}-m_X)+\varepsilon_t.\]

Multiply by \(X_{t-h}-m_X\), take expectations, and use \(E[\varepsilon_t(X_{t-h}-m_X)]=0\) for \(h\ge1\):

\[\gamma_X(h)=\phi_1\gamma_X(h-1)+\cdots+\phi_p\gamma_X(h-p),\qquad h\ge1.\]

Divide by \(\gamma_X(0)>0\): the Yule–Walker recurrence.

\[\boxed{\rho_X(h)=\phi_1\rho_X(h-1)+\cdots+\phi_p\rho_X(h-p),\quad h\ge1.}\]

1. Multiply by the lagged centred observation

\[\widetilde X_t=\sum_{i=1}^p\phi_i\widetilde X_{t-i}+\varepsilon_t,\]\[\widetilde X_t\widetilde X_{t-h}=\sum_{i=1}^p\phi_i\widetilde X_{t-i}\widetilde X_{t-h}+\varepsilon_t\widetilde X_{t-h}.\]

2. Take expectations and identify the lags

\[E(\widetilde X_t\widetilde X_{t-h})=\sum_{i=1}^p\phi_iE(\widetilde X_{t-i}\widetilde X_{t-h})+E(\varepsilon_t\widetilde X_{t-h}).\]
\[E(\widetilde X_{t-i}\widetilde X_{t-h})=\gamma_X(h-i)=\gamma_X(|h-i|).\]

Stationarity identifies the lag; symmetry handles negative lags.

3. Explain the zero cross-product

Causality gives \(\widetilde X_{t-h}=\sum_{k\ge0}\psi_k\varepsilon_{t-h-k}\). Since \(h+k\ge1\), every shock date is before \(t\):

\[E[\varepsilon_t(X_{t-h}-m_X)]=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\psi_k E(\varepsilon_t\varepsilon_{t-h-k})=0.\]

Different dates give zero covariance. Mean-square convergence permits the limit; independence is unnecessary.

4. Normalise each covariance

\[\gamma_X(h)=\sum_{i=1}^p\phi_i\gamma_X(h-i),\]\[\frac{\gamma_X(h)}{\gamma_X(0)}=\sum_{i=1}^p\phi_i\frac{\gamma_X(h-i)}{\gamma_X(0)},\]\[\rho_X(h)=\sum_{i=1}^p\phi_i\rho_X(h-i).\]

What the AR recurrence implies

\(h<p\): use \(\rho_X(-h)=\rho_X(h)\) and \(\rho_X(0)=1\) to find the initial values.

\(h\ge p\): a homogeneous difference equation of order \(p\), governed by the inverse AR roots.

\[|r_i|<1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad r_i^h\longrightarrow0.\]

The ACF tails off: exponential decay, alternating signs, or damped oscillations.

Try \(\rho_X(h)=Cr^h\), with \(C,r\ne0\), then divide by \(Cr^{h-p}\):

\[Cr^h=\phi_1Cr^{h-1}+\cdots+\phi_pCr^{h-p},\]\[r^p=\phi_1r^{p-1}+\cdots+\phi_p,\]\[r^p\Phi(1/r)=0.\]

The same inverse AR roots govern the ACF:

\[\rho_X(h)=\sum_i C_ir_i^h\quad\text{(distinct roots)}.\]\[h^m r_i^h\longrightarrow0\quad\text{if }|r_i|<1\quad\text{(repeated roots)}.\]

Negative roots alternate signs. For a complex-conjugate pair:

\[r_{1,2}=Re^{\pm i\omega},\quad R<1,\]\[C_1r_1^h+C_2r_2^h=R^h[A\cos(h\omega)+B\sin(h\omega)].\]

Oscillation is damped by the shrinking factor \(R^h\).

ACF of a stationary causal ARMA(p,q)

At early lags, the finite MA shocks can overlap with the past observation. Start from

\[\Phi(L)(X_t-m_X)=\Theta(L)\varepsilon_t,\]\[\widetilde X_t=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\psi_k\varepsilon_{t-k}.\]

\(\theta_j\): finite MA weights, \(\theta_0=1\).

\(\psi_k\): weights of \(\Theta(L)/\Phi(L)\), \(\psi_0=1\).

\(\sigma_\varepsilon^2\): variance contributed by a matching shock.

For positive lags there are two cases; separately, \(\rho_X(0)=1\):

\[\boxed{\rho_X(h)=\begin{cases}\begin{aligned}&\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^p\phi_i\rho_X(h-i)\\&\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\sigma_\varepsilon^2}{\gamma_X(0)}\sum_{j=0}^{q-h}\theta_{j+h}\psi_j\end{aligned},&1\le h\le q,\\[12pt]\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^p\phi_i\rho_X(h-i),&h>q.\end{cases}}\]

The correction measures shared shocks. For \(h>q\), no finite MA shock overlaps with \(X_{t-h}\), so

\[\boxed{\rho_X(h)=\phi_1\rho_X(h-1)+\cdots+\phi_p\rho_X(h-p),\quad h>q.}\]

The AR roots determine the tail; the MA part affects the initial correlations. A mixed ARMA ACF tails off.

1. Expand the centred model

\[\widetilde X_t=\sum_{i=1}^p\phi_i\widetilde X_{t-i}+\sum_{j=0}^q\theta_j\varepsilon_{t-j}.\]

Multiply by \(\widetilde X_{t-h}\) and take expectations:

\[\gamma_X(h)=\sum_{i=1}^p\phi_i\gamma_X(h-i)+\sum_{j=0}^q\theta_jE(\varepsilon_{t-j}\widetilde X_{t-h}).\]

2. Expand the earlier observation into shocks

\[\widetilde X_{t-h}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\psi_k\varepsilon_{t-h-k},\]\[E(\varepsilon_{t-j}\widetilde X_{t-h})=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\psi_kE(\varepsilon_{t-j}\varepsilon_{t-h-k}).\]
\[t-j=t-h-k\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad k=j-h\ge0.\]
\[E(\varepsilon_{t-j}\widetilde X_{t-h})=\begin{cases}\sigma_\varepsilon^2\psi_{j-h},&j\ge h,\\0,&j<h.\end{cases}\]

3. Sum the matching shocks

For \(1\le h\le q\), keep \(j=h,\ldots,q\):

\[\gamma_X(h)=\sum_{i=1}^p\phi_i\gamma_X(h-i)+\sigma_\varepsilon^2\sum_{j=h}^q\theta_j\psi_{j-h}.\]

Reindex with \(\ell=j-h\), so \(0\le\ell\le q-h\):

\[\sum_{j=h}^q\theta_j\psi_{j-h}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{q-h}\theta_{\ell+h}\psi_\ell.\]

\(h>q\): no \(j\le q\) can satisfy \(j\ge h\). The MA sum is zero.

4. Divide by the observation variance

\[\rho_X(h)=\sum_{i=1}^p\phi_i\rho_X(h-i)+\frac{\sigma_\varepsilon^2}{\gamma_X(0)}\sum_{\ell=0}^{q-h}\theta_{\ell+h}\psi_\ell\quad(1\le h\le q).\]

For \(h>q\), keep only the AR sum. The index \(\ell\) can be renamed \(j\).

The Yule–Walker matrix system

For a stationary causal AR\((p)\), stack the equations for \(h=1,\ldots,p\):

\[\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}1&\rho_X(1)&\cdots&\rho_X(p-1)\\\rho_X(1)&1&\cdots&\rho_X(p-2)\\\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\\rho_X(p-1)&\rho_X(p-2)&\cdots&1\end{bmatrix}}_{R_p}\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}\phi_1\\\phi_2\\\vdots\\\phi_p\end{bmatrix}}_{\boldsymbol\phi}=\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}\rho_X(1)\\\rho_X(2)\\\vdots\\\rho_X(p)\end{bmatrix}}_{\boldsymbol r_p}.\]
\[\boxed{R_p\boldsymbol\phi=\boldsymbol r_p.}\]
\[(R_p)_{hi}=\rho_X(|h-i|),\qquad(R_p)_{hh}=1.\]

\(R_p\): symmetric Toeplitz correlation matrix (constant diagonals). \(\boldsymbol\phi\): AR coefficients. \(\boldsymbol r_p\): first \(p\) correlations, not inverse roots.

Write the first two rows:

\[\rho_X(1)=\phi_1\rho_X(0)+\phi_2\rho_X(-1)+\cdots+\phi_p\rho_X(1-p),\]\[\rho_X(2)=\phi_1\rho_X(1)+\phi_2\rho_X(0)+\cdots+\phi_p\rho_X(2-p).\]

Use \(\rho_X(0)=1\) and \(\rho_X(-h)=\rho_X(h)\):

\[\text{Row 1: }(1,\rho_X(1),\ldots,\rho_X(p-1)),\]\[\text{Row 2: }(\rho_X(1),1,\ldots,\rho_X(p-2)).\]

Continue to row \(p\):

\[\sum_{i=1}^p\rho_X(h-i)\phi_i=\rho_X(h),\qquad h=1,\ldots,p.\]\[R_p\boldsymbol\phi=\boldsymbol r_p.\]

Example: the AR(2) Yule–Walker system

Take a stationary causal AR(2):

\[X_t=\phi_1X_{t-1}+\phi_2X_{t-2}+\varepsilon_t.\]

For \(h=1,2\):

\[\begin{bmatrix}1&\rho_X(1)\\\rho_X(1)&1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\phi_1\\\phi_2\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\rho_X(1)\\\rho_X(2)\end{bmatrix}.\]

Use \(\rho_X(-1)=\rho_X(1)\), \(\rho_X(0)=1\); solve row 1, then row 2:

\[\boxed{\rho_X(1)=\frac{\phi_1}{1-\phi_2},}\]\[\boxed{\rho_X(2)=\frac{\phi_1^2}{1-\phi_2}+\phi_2.}\]

Then continue recursively:

\[\boxed{\rho_X(h)=\phi_1\rho_X(h-1)+\phi_2\rho_X(h-2),\quad h\ge3.}\]

1. Solve the lag-one equation

\[\rho_X(1)=\phi_1\rho_X(0)+\phi_2\rho_X(-1),\]\[\rho_X(1)=\phi_1+\phi_2\rho_X(1),\]\[\rho_X(1)-\phi_2\rho_X(1)=\phi_1,\]\[(1-\phi_2)\rho_X(1)=\phi_1,\]\[\rho_X(1)=\frac{\phi_1}{1-\phi_2}.\]

Stability justifies the division:

\[|r_1|,|r_2|<1,\quad r_1r_2=-\phi_2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad|\phi_2|<1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad1-\phi_2\ne0.\]

2. Substitute into the lag-two equation

\[\begin{aligned}\rho_X(2)&=\phi_1\rho_X(1)+\phi_2\rho_X(0)\\&=\phi_1\left(\frac{\phi_1}{1-\phi_2}\right)+\phi_2\\&=\frac{\phi_1^2}{1-\phi_2}+\phi_2.\end{aligned}\]

3. Continue recursively

\[\begin{aligned}\rho_X(3)&=\phi_1\rho_X(2)+\phi_2\rho_X(1)\\&=\phi_1\left(\frac{\phi_1^2}{1-\phi_2}+\phi_2\right)+\phi_2\frac{\phi_1}{1-\phi_2},\end{aligned}\]\[\rho_X(4)=\phi_1\rho_X(3)+\phi_2\rho_X(2).\]

Repeat for later lags: the ACF tails off rather than cutting off after lag 2.

Part 5

Properties of the Partial Autocorrelation Function

Partial autocorrelation and linear projection

Use a centred process. Project \(X_t\) onto its first \(h\) lags:

\[X_t=a_{h1}X_{t-1}+\cdots+a_{hh}X_{t-h}+u_t^{(h)}.\]

\(a_{hj}\): coefficient on lag \(j\) in an \(h\)-lag projection. Least squares gives

\[E[u_t^{(h)}X_{t-j}]=0,\qquad j=1,\ldots,h.\]
\[\boxed{\alpha_X(h)=a_{hh}.}\]

The last coefficient measures dependence at lag \(h\), controlling for lags \(1,\ldots,h-1\). It need not equal the infinite-AR coefficient \(-b_h\).

PACF of an AR(p) process

A stationary causal AR\((p)\) uses only \(p\) lags to predict \(X_t\):

\[X_t=\phi_1X_{t-1}+\cdots+\phi_pX_{t-p}+\varepsilon_t.\]
\[E(\varepsilon_tX_{t-j})=0\quad(j\ge1).\]

After the first \(p\) lags, no additional lag improves the projection:

\[\boxed{\alpha_X(h)=\begin{cases}a_{hh},&1\le h\le p,\\0,&h>p.\end{cases}}\]

Cuts off after \(p\). At \(h=p\), \(a_{pp}=\phi_p\ne0\). For \(h<p\), omitted lags can change the coefficients.

1. Include at least the p model lags

For \(h>p\), consider the candidate projection coefficients

\[(a_{h1},\ldots,a_{hh})=(\phi_1,\ldots,\phi_p,0,\ldots,0).\]

Substituting them into the projection leaves exactly \(u_t^{(h)}=\varepsilon_t\).

2. Check the projection conditions

Check orthogonality to every included lag:

\[E(u_t^{(h)}X_{t-j})=E(\varepsilon_tX_{t-j})=0,\qquad j=1,\ldots,h.\]

These are the projection’s normal equations. For uniqueness, let \(j_0\) be the most recent lag with nonzero coefficient:

\[j_0=\min\{j:c_j\ne0\},\]\[\sum_{j=1}^h c_jX_{t-j}=c_{j_0}\varepsilon_{t-j_0}+\text{older-shock terms},\]\[\operatorname{Var}\!\left(\sum_{j=1}^h c_jX_{t-j}\right)\ge c_{j_0}^2\sigma_\varepsilon^2>0.\]

The covariance matrix is nonsingular, so the coefficients are unique.

3. Read the last coefficient

\[a_{hh}=0\quad(h>p),\qquad\alpha_X(h)=0\quad(h>p).\]

\(h=p\): \(a_{pp}=\phi_p\). For \(h<p\), do not replace \(a_{hh}\) by \(\phi_h\).

PACF of an MA(q) process

For a fundamental MA\((q)\), with \(\theta_0=1\):

\[X_t=\mu+\sum_{j=0}^q\theta_j\varepsilon_{t-j}.\]
\[\rho_X(h)=0\ (h>q)\quad\not\Rightarrow\quad\alpha_X(h)=0.\]

The PACF controls for intermediate observations; the ACF does not.

The causal inverse generally has infinitely many AR terms. Predictive dependence therefore tails off:

\[\boxed{\alpha_X(h)\longrightarrow0\quad\text{as }h\to\infty.}\]

No general finite cutoff. PACF coefficients are finite-projection coefficients, not the inverse-filter weights.

PACF of a causal and fundamental ARMA(p,q)

Assume both \(p,q>0\), no common roots, and all AR/MA roots outside the unit circle.

\[\frac{\Theta(L)}{\Phi(L)}:\ \operatorname{MA}(\infty),\qquad\frac{\Phi(L)}{\Theta(L)}:\ \operatorname{AR}(\infty).\]

Both dependence patterns generally persist: the ACF and PACF tail off.

\[\boxed{\alpha_X(h)\longrightarrow0\quad\text{as }h\to\infty.}\]

Pure AR and pure MA models retain their respective cutoff patterns below.

ACF and PACF identification

Cuts off after k

\[f(h)=0\quad\text{for every }h>k.\]

Tails off

\[f(h)\longrightarrow0\quad(h\to\infty).\]

Generally no finite cutoff.
