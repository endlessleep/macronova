LECTURE 3

ARMA Processes and Their Properties

Part 1

The ARMA model

Definition of an ARMA(p,q) process

An ARMA\((p,q)\) combines past observations and shocks:

\[\begin{aligned}X_t={}&\mu+\phi_1X_{t-1}+\cdots+\phi_pX_{t-p}\\&+\varepsilon_t+\theta_1\varepsilon_{t-1}+\cdots+\theta_q\varepsilon_{t-q}.\end{aligned}\]

AR part

\(p\) lags of \(X_t\); \(\phi_i\) multiplies \(X_{t-i}\). For \(p>0\), \(\phi_p\ne0\).

MA part

\(q\) lagged shocks; \(\theta_j\) multiplies \(\varepsilon_{t-j}\). For \(q>0\), \(\theta_q\ne0\).

Assume weak white noise with positive variance:

\[E(\varepsilon_t)=0,\qquad 0<\operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon_t)=\sigma_\varepsilon^2<\infty,\]\[E(\varepsilon_t\varepsilon_s)=0\quad(t\ne s).\]

\(\mu\) is the intercept; \(m_X=E(X_t)\) is the mean.

Lag-polynomial representation

\[L^iX_t=X_{t-i}.\]

Move the AR terms to the left; keep the shocks on the right:

\[\Phi(L)=1-\phi_1L-\cdots-\phi_pL^p,\]\[\Theta(L)=1+\theta_1L+\cdots+\theta_qL^q.\]

AR: minus signs. MA: original signs. Each coefficient may itself be positive or negative.

\[\boxed{\Phi(L)X_t=\mu+\Theta(L)\varepsilon_t.}\]

Move the AR terms left and use \(L^iX_t=X_{t-i}\):

\[X_t-\sum_{i=1}^p\phi_iX_{t-i}=\mu+\varepsilon_t+\sum_{j=1}^q\theta_j\varepsilon_{t-j},\]\[\left(1-\sum_{i=1}^p\phi_iL^i\right)X_t=\mu+\left(1+\sum_{j=1}^q\theta_jL^j\right)\varepsilon_t.\]\[\Phi(L)X_t=\mu+\Theta(L)\varepsilon_t.\]

AR(p) and MA(q) as special cases

Autoregressive process: AR(p)

\(q=0\), \(\Theta(L)=1\), and \(\phi_p\ne0\):

\[X_t=\mu+\phi_1X_{t-1}+\cdots+\phi_pX_{t-p}+\varepsilon_t.\]
\[\boxed{\Phi(L)X_t=\mu+\varepsilon_t.}\]

Moving-average process: MA(q)

\(p=0\), \(\Phi(L)=1\), and \(\theta_q\ne0\):

\[X_t=\mu+\varepsilon_t+\theta_1\varepsilon_{t-1}+\cdots+\theta_q\varepsilon_{t-q}.\]
\[\boxed{X_t=\mu+\Theta(L)\varepsilon_t.}\]

The current-shock coefficient is 1. MA weights need not sum to 1.

The intercept and the mean

For a stationary solution, the mean \(m_X=E(X_t)\) is:

\[\boxed{m_X=\frac{\mu}{1-\sum_{i=1}^p\phi_i}=\frac{\mu}{\Phi(1)}.}\]

\(\Phi(1)=1-\sum_{i=1}^p\phi_i\). For an MA, \(\Phi(1)=1\), so \(m_X=\mu\).

Take expectations: \(E(X_{t-i})=m_X\) and \(E(\varepsilon_{t-j})=0\).

\[m_X=\mu+\sum_{i=1}^p\phi_i m_X,\]\[m_X\left(1-\sum_{i=1}^p\phi_i\right)=\mu,\]\[m_X=\frac{\mu}{\Phi(1)}\qquad\bigl(\Phi(1)\ne0\bigr).\]

Mean-adjusted ARMA

Subtract the mean:

\[\widetilde X_t=X_t-m_X,\qquad E(\widetilde X_t)=m_X-m_X=0.\]
\[\boxed{\widetilde X_t=X_t-\frac{\mu}{1-\sum_{i=1}^p\phi_i}=X_t-\frac{\mu}{\Phi(1)}.}\]\[\boxed{\Phi(L)\widetilde X_t=\Theta(L)\varepsilon_t.}\]

Centring removes the intercept and leaves the AR/MA coefficients, covariances and correlations unchanged.

Substitute \(X_t=\widetilde X_t+m_X\), using \(\Phi(1)m_X=\mu\):

\[\Phi(L)(\widetilde X_t+m_X)=\mu+\Theta(L)\varepsilon_t,\]\[\Phi(L)\widetilde X_t+\Phi(1)m_X=\mu+\Theta(L)\varepsilon_t,\]\[\Phi(L)\widetilde X_t=\underbrace{\mu-\Phi(1)m_X}_{0}+\Theta(L)\varepsilon_t=\Theta(L)\varepsilon_t.\]
Part 2

Root conditions

Check \(\Phi\) for stationarity and stability; check \(\Theta\) for invertibility.

Unit circle and root conventions

\[z=a+ib,\qquad |z|=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}.\]

The unit circle contains points at distance 1 from zero:

\[\mathcal C=\{z\in\mathbb C:\ |z|=1\}.\]

Assumption: \(\Phi\) and \(\Theta\) have no common roots, so no AR/MA factor cancels.

A root makes its polynomial zero. Substitute \(z=1/r\) and multiply by \(r^p\):

\[\Phi(z)=1-\phi_1z-\cdots-\phi_pz^p=0,\]\[r^p\Phi(1/r)=r^p-\phi_1r^{p-1}-\cdots-\phi_p=0.\]
\[r_i=\frac1{z_i},\qquad |r_i|=\frac1{|z_i|}.\]

Outside roots become inside inverse roots; unit-circle roots stay on the circle.

If \(\Phi(z_i)=0\), substitute \(r_i=1/z_i\):

\[r_i^p\Phi(1/r_i)=r_i^p\Phi(z_i)=0,\]\[|r_i|=\frac1{|z_i|},\]\[|z_i|>1\iff|r_i|<1,\qquad |z_i|\ne1\iff|r_i|\ne1.\]
2.1

Stationarity

Condition

Check the roots of the AR polynomial \(\Phi(z)=0\). They may be inside or outside the unit circle, but none may be on it:

\[\boxed{\Phi(z_i)=0\implies |z_i|\ne1\quad\text{for every }i.}\]\[\boxed{r_i^p\Phi(1/r_i)=0\implies |r_i|\ne1\quad\text{for every }i.}\]

What does it allow us to do?

A stationary solution exists and is unique. We can write it as mean + weighted shocks.

Theorem — stationary representation

Centre the equation, then invert \(\Phi(L)\):

\[\Phi(L)(X_t-m_X)=\Theta(L)\varepsilon_t.\]
\[\boxed{X_t=\underbrace{m_X}_{\text{mean}}+\underbrace{\Psi(L)\varepsilon_t}_{\text{weighted shocks}},}\]\[\Psi(L)=\frac{\Theta(L)}{\Phi(L)},\qquad m_X=\frac{\mu}{\Phi(1)}.\]
Bilateral representation: past and future shocks

Both signs of \(j\) are allowed; \(L^j\varepsilon_t=\varepsilon_{t-j}\):

\[\Psi(L)=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\psi_jL^j.\]
\(\underbrace{j=1:\ \varepsilon_{t-1}}_{\text{past}}\qquad\)\(\underbrace{j=0:\ \varepsilon_t}_{\text{present}}\qquad\)\(\underbrace{j=-1:\ \varepsilon_{t+1}}_{\text{future}}\)

All AR roots \(|z_i|>1\): current and past shocks only (\(\psi_j=0\) for \(j<0\)).

At least one \(|z_i|<1\), none \(|z_i|=1\): stationary, but future shocks are needed.

Any AR root \(|z_i|=1\): no stationary solution.

Why every finite MA(q) is stationary
\[\Phi(L)=1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad X_t=\mu+\sum_{j=0}^{q}\theta_j\varepsilon_{t-j},\qquad\theta_0=1.\]

Fixed weights give a constant mean and covariances depending only on the lag: every finite MA is stationary, regardless of its roots.

2.2

Stability

Condition

Check the same AR roots, now requiring all of them to be outside the unit circle. Equivalently, their inverse roots \(r_i=1/z_i\) must be inside:

\[\boxed{|z_i|>1\iff |r_i|<1\quad\text{for every AR root}.}\]

What does it allow us to do?

From shocks to observations: express \(X_t\) using \(\varepsilon_t,\varepsilon_{t-1},\ldots\). This is the causal MA(∞) representation:

\[\Psi(L)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\psi_jL^j.\]
\[\boxed{X_t=m_X+\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\psi_j\varepsilon_{t-j}.}\]

Stability implies stationarity. It also ensures that the representation uses no future shocks.

2.3

Invertibility

Condition

For stationary \(X_t\), check the roots of the MA polynomial \(\Theta(z)=0\). They must all be outside the unit circle:

\[\boxed{\Theta(z_i)=0\implies |z_i|>1\quad\text{for every MA root}.}\]

What does it allow us to do?

From observations to shocks: recover \(\varepsilon_t\) using \(X_t,X_{t-1},\ldots\). No future observations are needed.

\[\boxed{\varepsilon_t=m_X^*+\underbrace{\frac{\Phi(L)}{\Theta(L)}X_t}_{b_0X_t+b_1X_{t-1}+b_2X_{t-2}+\cdots}.}\]\[m_X^*=-\frac{\mu}{\Theta(1)}=-\frac{\Phi(1)}{\Theta(1)}m_X.\]

\(m_X^*\) is the constant that makes \(E(\varepsilon_t)=0\).

Isolate the shock term, then apply \(\Theta(L)^{-1}\):

\[\Theta(L)\varepsilon_t=\Phi(L)X_t-\mu,\]\[\varepsilon_t=\frac{\Phi(L)}{\Theta(L)}X_t-c,\qquad \Theta(L)c=\mu.\]

To find the constant \(c\), use \(Lc=L^2c=\cdots=c\):

\[\begin{aligned}\Theta(L)c&=c+\theta_1c+\cdots+\theta_qc\\&=\underbrace{(1+\theta_1+\cdots+\theta_q)}_{\Theta(1)}c=\mu\\&\Longrightarrow\quad c=\frac{\mu}{\Theta(1)}.\end{aligned}\]

Substitute \(c\); the constant in the recovered-shock equation is \(m_X^*=-c\):

\[\varepsilon_t=\underbrace{-\frac{\mu}{\Theta(1)}}_{m_X^*}+\frac{\Phi(L)}{\Theta(L)}X_t.\]

Part 3

Representations and examples

Causal = stable. Fundamental = invertible. Each name describes a different direction:

Causal: shocks → observations

\[\varepsilon_t,\varepsilon_{t-1},\ldots\ \longrightarrow\ X_t.\]

Use current and past shocks. Stability: all AR roots are outside the unit circle.

Fundamental: observations → shocks

\[X_t,X_{t-1},\ldots\ \longrightarrow\ \varepsilon_t.\]

Recover shocks from current and past observations. The root conditions are:

\[\text{AR roots: }|z_i|\ne1\quad\text{(stationarity)},\]\[\text{MA roots: }|z_i|>1\quad\text{(invertibility)}.\]

Causal and fundamental: both directions

Both hold when all AR and MA roots are outside the unit circle:

\[\boxed{\begin{aligned}\Phi(z_i)=0&\implies |z_i|>1\quad\text{for every AR root},\\\Theta(z_i)=0&\implies |z_i|>1\quad\text{for every MA root}.\end{aligned}}\]

Proposition — two infinite representations

For a causal and fundamental model, the two directions give:

MA(∞): calculate \(X_t\)

\[\boxed{X_t=m_X+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_k\varepsilon_{t-k}.}\]

\(a_k\) weights the shock \(k\) periods ago; \(a_k=\psi_k\):

\[\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_kL^k=\frac{\Theta(L)}{\Phi(L)}.\]

AR(∞): calculate \(\varepsilon_t\)

\[\boxed{\varepsilon_t=m_X^*+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}b_kX_{t-k}.}\]

\(b_k\) weights the observation \(k\) periods ago:

\[\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}b_kL^k=\frac{\Phi(L)}{\Theta(L)}.\]

The shocks are innovations

These shocks \(\{\varepsilon_t\}_{t\in\mathbb Z}\) are the one-step prediction errors:

\[\underbrace{\varepsilon_t}_{\text{innovation}}=\underbrace{X_t}_{\text{observed}}-\underbrace{\widehat X_{t\mid t-1}}_{\text{predicted}}.\]

\(\widehat X_{t\mid t-1}\) is the best linear prediction using \(X_{t-1},X_{t-2},\ldots\).

AR(1): from its root to its representation

Take the zero-intercept model with Gaussian white noise:

\[(1-\rho L)X_t=\varepsilon_t,\qquad \varepsilon_t\overset{iid}{\sim}\mathcal N(0,\sigma_\varepsilon^2).\]

For \(\rho\ne0\), solve the characteristic equation:

\[\Phi(z)=1-\rho z=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad z_1=\frac1\rho.\]
\[\text{Stationarity: }|z_1|\ne1\iff|\rho|\ne1,\]\[\text{Stability: }|z_1|>1\iff|\rho|<1.\]

\(\rho=0\Rightarrow X_t=\varepsilon_t\): stationary and causal, with no AR root.

Case 1: |ρ| < 1 — stationary and causal

\(|\rho|<1\): geometric weights decay. Also \(\Theta(L)=1\), so the representation is causal and fundamental.

\[\Phi(L)^{-1}=\frac1{1-\rho L}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\rho^kL^k,\]\[\boxed{X_t=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\rho^k\varepsilon_{t-k}.}\]

For \(|\rho|<1\), use the geometric expansion and \(L^k\varepsilon_t=\varepsilon_{t-k}\):

\[(1-\rho L)^{-1}=1+\rho L+\rho^2L^2+\cdots,\]\[X_t=(1+\rho L+\rho^2L^2+\cdots)\varepsilon_t,\]\[X_t=\varepsilon_t+\rho\varepsilon_{t-1}+\rho^2\varepsilon_{t-2}+\cdots=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\rho^k\varepsilon_{t-k}.\]

Case 2: |ρ| > 1 — stationary and noncausal

The expansion in past shocks does not converge. Since \(|\rho^{-1}|<1\), expand in future shocks:

\[F=L^{-1},\qquad F^k\varepsilon_t=\varepsilon_{t+k},\qquad |\rho^{-1}|<1.\]
\[\Phi(L)^{-1}=-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\rho^{-k}F^k,\]\[\boxed{X_t=-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\rho^{-k}\varepsilon_{t+k}.}\]

Noncausal: \(X_t\) uses future shocks. Fundamental: recover the shock as \(\varepsilon_t=X_t-\rho X_{t-1}\).

Use \(F=L^{-1}\), \(LF=1\), and \(|\rho^{-1}|<1\) to expand in leads:

\[1-\rho L=-\rho L(1-\rho^{-1}F),\]\[(1-\rho L)^{-1}=-\rho^{-1}F(1-\rho^{-1}F)^{-1},\]\[\begin{aligned}(1-\rho L)^{-1}&=-\rho^{-1}F\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\rho^{-j}F^j\\&=-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\rho^{-k}F^k,\qquad k=j+1,\end{aligned}\]\[X_t=-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\rho^{-k}F^k\varepsilon_t=-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\rho^{-k}\varepsilon_{t+k}.\]

Case 3: |ρ| = 1 — no stationary solution

Unit-circle root; neither lag nor lead weights decay. No stationary finite-variance solution; no stability.

MA(1): check stationarity and invertibility

Take the finite moving average:

\[X_t=\varepsilon_t+\theta\varepsilon_{t-1},\qquad\varepsilon_t\overset{iid}{\sim}\mathcal N(0,\sigma_\varepsilon^2).\]

For \(\theta\ne0\), calculate the MA root:

\[\Theta(z)=1+\theta z=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\theta z=-1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad z_1=-\frac1\theta.\]
\[\boxed{\text{Stationary regardless of the MA root.}}\]\[\boxed{\text{Invertible: }|z_1|>1\iff\frac1{|\theta|}>1\iff|\theta|<1.}\]

If \(\theta=0\), \(X_t=\varepsilon_t\): white noise, with no MA root to check.

Part 4

ACF: correlation across lags

The ACF measures the correlation between \(X_t\) and \(X_{t-h}\). For stationary \(X_t\) with positive variance:

\[\gamma_X(h)=E[(X_t-\mu_X)(X_{t-h}-\mu_X)],\]\[\rho_X(h)=\frac{\gamma_X(h)}{\gamma_X(0)},\qquad \rho_X(0)=1.\]

\(\gamma_X(h)\): autocovariance; \(\gamma_X(0)\): variance. Here \(\mu_X=m_X=E(X_t)\) is the mean, not the intercept \(\mu\).

MA(q): the ACF stops after lag q

For a fundamental MA\((q)\), set \(\theta_0=1\):

\[X_t=\mu+\sum_{j=0}^q\theta_j\varepsilon_{t-j},\qquad \widetilde X_t=\sum_{j=0}^q\theta_j\varepsilon_{t-j}.\]

Match shock dates. Different dates contribute zero:

\[\gamma_X(0)=\sigma_\varepsilon^2\sum_{j=0}^q\theta_j^2,\]\[\gamma_X(h)=\sigma_\varepsilon^2\sum_{j=0}^{q-h}\theta_j\theta_{j+h}\quad(1\le h\le q).\]
\[\boxed{\rho_X(h)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{j=0}^{q-h}\theta_j\theta_{j+h}}{\sum_{j=0}^q\theta_j^2},&1\le h\le q,\\[8pt]0,&h>q.\end{cases}}\]

\(\sigma_\varepsilon^2\) cancels. For \(h>q\), no common shock remains: the ACF cuts off after \(q\).

At \(h=q\), only \(\theta_0\theta_q=\theta_q\) remains. Use symmetry for negative lags:

\[\rho_X(q)=\frac{\theta_q}{\sum_{j=0}^q\theta_j^2},\qquad \rho_X(-h)=\rho_X(h).\]

Only equal shock dates contribute \(\sigma_\varepsilon^2\); all other expected products are zero. For \(1\le h\le q\):

\[\gamma_X(h)=\sum_{j=0}^q\sum_{k=0}^q\theta_j\theta_kE(\varepsilon_{t-j}\varepsilon_{t-h-k}),\]\[t-j=t-h-k\iff j=h+k,\qquad 0\le k\le q-h,\]\[\gamma_X(h)=\sigma_\varepsilon^2\sum_{k=0}^{q-h}\theta_{h+k}\theta_k,\qquad\gamma_X(0)=\sigma_\varepsilon^2\sum_{j=0}^q\theta_j^2,\]\[\rho_X(h)=\frac{\sigma_\varepsilon^2\sum_{k=0}^{q-h}\theta_{h+k}\theta_k}{\sigma_\varepsilon^2\sum_{j=0}^q\theta_j^2}=\frac{\sum_{k=0}^{q-h}\theta_{h+k}\theta_k}{\sum_{j=0}^q\theta_j^2}.\]

If \(h>q\), no dates match, so \(\gamma_X(h)=\rho_X(h)=0\).

AR(p): the ACF follows a recurrence

Assume causality: current shocks are orthogonal to past observations. Centre the AR equation:

\[X_t-\mu_X=\phi_1(X_{t-1}-\mu_X)+\cdots+\phi_p(X_{t-p}-\mu_X)+\varepsilon_t.\]

Multiply by \(X_{t-h}-\mu_X\), take expectations, and use \(E[\varepsilon_t(X_{t-h}-\mu_X)]=0\) for \(h\ge1\):

\[\gamma_X(h)=\phi_1\gamma_X(h-1)+\cdots+\phi_p\gamma_X(h-p),\qquad h\ge1.\]

Divide by \(\gamma_X(0)>0\): the Yule–Walker recurrence.

\[\boxed{\rho_X(h)=\phi_1\rho_X(h-1)+\cdots+\phi_p\rho_X(h-p),\quad h\ge1.}\]

For \(h\ge1\), causality makes \(\widetilde X_{t-h}\) depend only on past shocks, uncorrelated with \(\varepsilon_t\). Multiply and take expectations:

\[\widetilde X_t\widetilde X_{t-h}=\sum_{i=1}^p\phi_i\widetilde X_{t-i}\widetilde X_{t-h}+\varepsilon_t\widetilde X_{t-h},\]\[\underbrace{E(\widetilde X_t\widetilde X_{t-h})}_{\gamma_X(h)}=\sum_{i=1}^p\phi_i\underbrace{E(\widetilde X_{t-i}\widetilde X_{t-h})}_{\gamma_X(h-i)}+\underbrace{E(\varepsilon_t\widetilde X_{t-h})}_{0},\]\[\gamma_X(h)=\sum_{i=1}^p\phi_i\gamma_X(h-i),\]\[\rho_X(h)=\frac{\gamma_X(h)}{\gamma_X(0)}=\sum_{i=1}^p\phi_i\frac{\gamma_X(h-i)}{\gamma_X(0)}=\sum_{i=1}^p\phi_i\rho_X(h-i).\]

Read the recurrence

\(h<p\): use \(\rho_X(-h)=\rho_X(h)\) and \(\rho_X(0)=1\) to find the initial values.

\(h\ge p\): a homogeneous difference equation of order \(p\), governed by the inverse AR roots.

The ACF tails off geometrically, possibly with alternating signs or damped oscillations.

AR(p): calculate the first autocorrelations with Yule–Walker

For a stationary causal AR\((p)\), stack the equations for \(h=1,\ldots,p\):

\[\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}1&\rho_X(1)&\cdots&\rho_X(p-1)\\\rho_X(1)&1&\cdots&\rho_X(p-2)\\\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\\rho_X(p-1)&\rho_X(p-2)&\cdots&1\end{bmatrix}}_{R_p}\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}\phi_1\\\phi_2\\\vdots\\\phi_p\end{bmatrix}}_{\boldsymbol\phi}=\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}\rho_X(1)\\\rho_X(2)\\\vdots\\\rho_X(p)\end{bmatrix}}_{\boldsymbol r_p}.\]
\[\boxed{R_p\boldsymbol\phi=\boldsymbol r_p.}\]
\[(R_p)_{hi}=\rho_X(|h-i|),\qquad(R_p)_{hh}=1.\]

\(R_p\) contains correlations; \(\boldsymbol\phi\) contains AR coefficients; \(\boldsymbol r_p\) contains the first \(p\) autocorrelations.

Use \(\rho_X(0)=1\) and \(\rho_X(-h)=\rho_X(h)\). The first equations become the first matrix rows:

\[\rho_X(1)=\phi_1+\phi_2\rho_X(1)+\cdots+\phi_p\rho_X(p-1),\]\[\rho_X(2)=\phi_1\rho_X(1)+\phi_2+\cdots+\phi_p\rho_X(p-2),\]\[\sum_{i=1}^p\rho_X(|h-i|)\phi_i=\rho_X(h)\quad(h=1,\ldots,p)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad R_p\boldsymbol\phi=\boldsymbol r_p.\]

AR(2): solve the first two lags, then continue

Take a stationary causal AR(2):

\[X_t=\phi_1X_{t-1}+\phi_2X_{t-2}+\varepsilon_t.\]

For \(h=1,2\):

\[\begin{bmatrix}1&\rho_X(1)\\\rho_X(1)&1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\phi_1\\\phi_2\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\rho_X(1)\\\rho_X(2)\end{bmatrix}.\]

Use \(\rho_X(-1)=\rho_X(1)\), \(\rho_X(0)=1\); solve row 1, then row 2:

\[\boxed{\rho_X(1)=\frac{\phi_1}{1-\phi_2},}\]\[\boxed{\rho_X(2)=\frac{\phi_1^2}{1-\phi_2}+\phi_2.}\]

Then continue recursively:

\[\boxed{\rho_X(h)=\phi_1\rho_X(h-1)+\phi_2\rho_X(h-2),\quad h\ge3.}\]

Use \(\rho_X(0)=1\) and \(\rho_X(-1)=\rho_X(1)\):

\[\rho_X(1)=\phi_1+\phi_2\rho_X(1),\]\[(1-\phi_2)\rho_X(1)=\phi_1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\rho_X(1)=\frac{\phi_1}{1-\phi_2},\]\[\rho_X(2)=\phi_1\rho_X(1)+\phi_2,\]\[\rho_X(2)=\phi_1\frac{\phi_1}{1-\phi_2}+\phi_2=\frac{\phi_1^2}{1-\phi_2}+\phi_2.\]

ARMA(p,q): include the MA effect at the first lags

At early lags, the finite MA shocks can overlap with the past observation. Start from

\[\Phi(L)(X_t-\mu_X)=\Theta(L)\varepsilon_t,\]\[\widetilde X_t=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\psi_k\varepsilon_{t-k}.\]

\(\theta_j\): finite MA weights, \(\theta_0=1\).

\(\psi_k\): weights of \(\Theta(L)/\Phi(L)\), \(\psi_0=1\).

\(\sigma_\varepsilon^2\): variance contributed by a matching shock.

For positive lags there are two cases; separately, \(\rho_X(0)=1\):

\[\boxed{\rho_X(h)=\begin{cases}\begin{aligned}&\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^p\phi_i\rho_X(h-i)\\&\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\sigma_\varepsilon^2}{\gamma_X(0)}\sum_{j=0}^{q-h}\theta_{j+h}\psi_j\end{aligned},&1\le h\le q,\\[12pt]\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^p\phi_i\rho_X(h-i),&h>q.\end{cases}}\]

The correction measures shared shocks. For \(h>q\), no finite MA shock overlaps with \(X_{t-h}\), so

\[\boxed{\rho_X(h)=\phi_1\rho_X(h-1)+\cdots+\phi_p\rho_X(h-p),\quad h>q.}\]

The AR roots determine the tail; the MA part affects the initial correlations. A mixed ARMA ACF tails off.

Expand \(\widetilde X_{t-h}=\sum_{k\ge0}\psi_k\varepsilon_{t-h-k}\). Shock dates match only if \(j=h+k\).

\[\gamma_X(h)=\sum_{i=1}^p\phi_i\gamma_X(h-i)+\sum_{j=0}^q\theta_jE(\varepsilon_{t-j}\widetilde X_{t-h}),\]\[E(\varepsilon_{t-j}\widetilde X_{t-h})=\begin{cases}\sigma_\varepsilon^2\psi_{j-h},&j\ge h,\\0,&j<h,\end{cases}\]\[\sum_{j=h}^q\theta_j\psi_{j-h}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{q-h}\theta_{\ell+h}\psi_\ell\qquad(\ell=j-h),\]\[\rho_X(h)=\sum_{i=1}^p\phi_i\rho_X(h-i)+\frac{\sigma_\varepsilon^2}{\gamma_X(0)}\sum_{\ell=0}^{q-h}\theta_{\ell+h}\psi_\ell\quad(1\le h\le q).\]

The last line divides by \(\gamma_X(0)\). If \(h>q\), no \(j\le q\) can match, so only the AR sum remains.

Part 5

PACF and model identification

PACF: the coefficient on the last lag

For a centred process, regress \(X_t\) on \(X_{t-1},\ldots,X_{t-h}\):

\[X_t=a_{h1}X_{t-1}+\cdots+a_{hh}X_{t-h}+u_t^{(h)}.\]

\(a_{hj}\) is the coefficient on lag \(j\) in the \(h\)-lag projection; \(u_t^{(h)}\) is the prediction error.

\[\boxed{\alpha_X(h)=a_{hh}.}\]

At lag \(h\), read the coefficient on \(X_{t-h}\) after including \(X_{t-1},\ldots,X_{t-h+1}\). For \(h=1\), there are no intermediate lags.

MA(q): the PACF tails off

For a stationary process with a fundamental MA\((q)\) representation, \(\theta_0=1\):

\[X_t=\mu+\sum_{j=0}^q\theta_j\varepsilon_{t-j}.\]

Its PACF tails off toward zero:

\[\boxed{\alpha_X(h)\longrightarrow0\quad\text{as }h\to\infty.}\]

AR(p): the PACF stops after lag p

For a stationary causal AR\((p)\),

\[X_t=\phi_1X_{t-1}+\cdots+\phi_pX_{t-p}+\varepsilon_t.\]
\[\boxed{\alpha_X(h)=\begin{cases}a_{hh},&1\le h\le p,\\0,&h>p.\end{cases}}\]

The PACF cuts off after \(p\): once the first \(p\) lags are included, more distant lags add no linear predictive information.

ARMA(p,q): the PACF tails off

For a causal and fundamental ARMA\((p,q)\) with \(p,q>0\), the PACF tails off toward zero:

\[\boxed{\alpha_X(h)\longrightarrow0\quad\text{as }h\to\infty.}\]

Identify the model from the ACF and PACF

Cuts off after k

\[f(h)=0\quad\text{for every }h>k.\]

Tails off

\[f(h)\longrightarrow0\quad(h\to\infty).\]

Generally no finite cutoff.
