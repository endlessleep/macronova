PROBLEM SET 2

What to find · Formula

AR(2) and MA(2): when are ACF and PACF zero?

AR(2)
ACF: \(\rho_X(h)\) can still be different from zero at lags 3, 4, and beyond.
PACF: \(\alpha_X(h)=0\) for every \(h>2\). Only lags 1 and 2 need to be calculated.

MA(2)
ACF: \(\rho_Y(h)=0\) for every \(h>2\). Only lags 1 and 2 need to be calculated, with \(\rho_Y(0)=1\).
PACF: \(\alpha_Y(h)\) can still be different from zero at lags 3, 4, and beyond. Calculate each requested lag.

What to find · Formula

AR and MA: which coefficient must be 1?

These forms are written for a zero-mean or demeaned process.

AR(\(p\)): by convention, the coefficient in front of \(X_t\) is 1.

\(X_t=\phi_1X_{t-1}+\cdots\)\({}+\phi_pX_{t-p}+\varepsilon_t.\)

MA(\(q\)): by convention, the coefficient in front of \(\varepsilon_t\) is 1.

\(X_t=\varepsilon_t+\theta_1\varepsilon_{t-1}\)\({}+\cdots+\theta_q\varepsilon_{t-q}.\)
Exercise 1

An AR(2) process

Consider

\[X_t=\frac16-\frac16X_{t-1}+\frac16X_{t-2}+\varepsilon_t,\]

where \(\varepsilon_t\) is a weak white noise with variance \(\sigma_\varepsilon^2\).

  1. Give the mean of \(X_t\), and write the demeaned process \(\widetilde X_t\).
  2. Show that \(X_t\) is stationary and give its MA\((\infty)\) representation.
  3. Compute the ACF up to \(h=3\).
  4. Compute the PACF up to \(h=3\).

Given information & notation

\[E(\varepsilon_t)=0,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon_t)=\sigma_\varepsilon^2,\qquad \operatorname{Cov}(\varepsilon_t,\varepsilon_{t-h})=0\ (h\ne0).\]

Write \(\widetilde X_t=X_t-\mu_X\). The lag operator satisfies \(LX_t=X_{t-1}\).

Question 1 Mean and demeaned process

What to find · Formula

Under stationarity, the mean is the same at every date. Then centre the process around that mean.

\[\mu_X=E(X_t),\qquad \widetilde X_t=X_t-\mu_X.\]
\[\mu_X=\frac16-\frac16\mu_X+\frac16\mu_X+E(\varepsilon_t)=\frac16.\]
\[\boxed{\mu_X=\frac16.}\]

Demeaned process

We know that \(\mu_X=\frac16\). Subtract \(\frac16\) directly from both sides of the model:

\[X_t-\frac16=-\frac16X_{t-1}+\frac16X_{t-2}+\varepsilon_t.\]

Since \(\widetilde X_t=X_t-\frac16\), substitute

\[X_{t-1}=\widetilde X_{t-1}+\frac16,\qquad X_{t-2}=\widetilde X_{t-2}+\frac16,\]
\[\widetilde X_t=-\frac16\left(\widetilde X_{t-1}+\frac16\right)+\frac16\left(\widetilde X_{t-2}+\frac16\right)+\varepsilon_t.\]

The constant terms cancel: \(-\frac1{36}+\frac1{36}=0\). Therefore:

\[\boxed{\widetilde X_t=-\frac16\widetilde X_{t-1}+\frac16\widetilde X_{t-2}+\varepsilon_t.}\]

Question 2 Stationarity and MA\((\infty)\)

What to find · Formula

Calculate the inverse roots \(r_1,r_2\), then use them in the factors \((1-r_1L)(1-r_2L)\). Inverse roots inside the unit circle make this AR(2) stationary and causal.

\[\Phi(L)\widetilde X_t=\varepsilon_t,\qquad r^2\Phi(r^{-1})=0,\qquad |r_i|<1.\]

Using \(L\), the demeaned process becomes

\[\left(1+\frac16L-\frac16L^2\right)\widetilde X_t=\varepsilon_t,\qquad \Phi(L)=1+\frac16L-\frac16L^2.\]

Inverse roots

Replace \(L\) by \(r^{-1}\), then multiply by \(r^2\) to remove the negative powers:

\[r^2\Phi(r^{-1})=0\] \[r^2\left(1+\frac16r^{-1}-\frac16r^{-2}\right)=0\] \[r^2+\frac16r-\frac16=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad6r^2+r-1=0.\]

Use the quadratic formula:

\[\Delta=1^2-4(6)(-1)=25,\qquad r=\frac{-1\pm\sqrt{25}}{12}.\] \[r_1=\frac{-1+5}{12}=\frac13,\qquad r_2=\frac{-1-5}{12}=-\frac12.\]
\[\boxed{r_1=\frac13,\qquad r_2=-\frac12.}\] \[|r_1|=\frac13<1,\qquad |r_2|=\frac12<1.\]

Both inverse roots lie inside the unit circle, so the AR(2) is stationary and causal.

Factorization and partial fractions

Use the inverse roots in \((1-r_1L)(1-r_2L)\), then find the partial-fraction coefficients:

\[\Phi(L)=(1-r_1L)(1-r_2L)\] \[=\left(1-\frac13L\right)\left(1+\frac12L\right),\] \[\frac{1}{(1-\frac13L)(1+\frac12L)}=\frac{C_1}{1-\frac13L}+\frac{C_2}{1+\frac12L}.\]

Multiply by the common denominator, then group constants and coefficients of \(L\):

\[\begin{aligned}1&=C_1\left(1+\frac12L\right)+C_2\left(1-\frac13L\right)\\&=(C_1+C_2)+\left(\frac{C_1}{2}-\frac{C_2}{3}\right)L.\end{aligned}\]

The left side is \(1+0L\), so

\[\begin{cases}C_1+C_2=1,\\\frac{C_1}{2}-\frac{C_2}{3}=0.\end{cases}\qquad C_2=\frac32C_1\quad\Rightarrow\quad\frac52C_1=1.\] \[\boxed{C_1=\frac25,\qquad C_2=\frac35.}\] \[\Phi(L)^{-1}=\frac25\left(1-\frac13L\right)^{-1}+\frac35\left(1+\frac12L\right)^{-1}.\]
\[\Phi(L)^{-1}=\frac25\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac13\right)^jL^j+\frac35\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\left(-\frac12\right)^jL^j.\]

Apply the inverse polynomial to \(\varepsilon_t\):

\[\boxed{\widetilde X_t=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\left[\frac25\left(\frac13\right)^j+\frac35\left(-\frac12\right)^j\right]\varepsilon_{t-j}.}\]

Question 3 ACF up to lag 3

What to find · Formula

Use the AR(2) recursion with \(\phi_1=-\frac16\), \(\phi_2=\frac16\), and \(\rho_X(0)=1\).

\[\rho_X(h)=\phi_1\rho_X(h-1)+\phi_2\rho_X(h-2).\]

Multiply the demeaned AR(2) equation by \(\widetilde X_{t-h}\) and take expectations. For \(h\geq1\), \(E(\varepsilon_t\widetilde X_{t-h})=0\), so

\[\begin{aligned}E(\widetilde X_t\widetilde X_{t-h})&=-\frac16E(\widetilde X_{t-1}\widetilde X_{t-h})\\&\quad+\frac16E(\widetilde X_{t-2}\widetilde X_{t-h}).\end{aligned}\] \[\gamma_X(h)=-\frac16\gamma_X(h-1)+\frac16\gamma_X(h-2).\]

Divide the entire equation by \(\gamma_X(0)\):

\[\rho_X(h)=-\frac16\rho_X(h-1)+\frac16\rho_X(h-2),\qquad h\geq1.\]

At lag one, use \(\rho_X(-1)=\rho_X(1)\):

\[\rho_X(1)=\phi_1+\phi_2\rho_X(1),\]\[\rho_X(1)(1-\phi_2)=\phi_1,\]\[\rho_X(1)=\frac{-1/6}{1-1/6}=-\frac15.\]

Then apply the recursion at lags two and three:

\[\rho_X(2)=-\frac16\left(-\frac15\right)+\frac16(1)=\frac1{30}+\frac5{30}=\frac15,\]\[\rho_X(3)=-\frac16\left(\frac15\right)+\frac16\left(-\frac15\right)=-\frac1{15}.\]
AR(2) ACF up to lag 3
Lag \(h\)0123
\(\rho_X(h)\)\(1\)\(-\frac15\)\(\frac15\)\(-\frac1{15}\)

Question 4 PACF up to lag 3

\(h=1\)

\[R_1a_1=\rho_1.\]
\[a_1=\begin{pmatrix}a_{11}\end{pmatrix}\]
\[\rho_1=\begin{pmatrix}\rho_X(1)\end{pmatrix}.\]

We want the last coefficient: \(\operatorname{PACF}(1)=\alpha_X(1)=a_{11}\). Insert the ACF values into the system:

\[\begin{pmatrix}1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a_{11}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}-\frac15\end{pmatrix}.\]

Use Cramer’s rule for \(a_{11}\): replace the first (and last) column of \(R_1\) by \(\rho_1\) to form \(R_1^\star\).

\[a_{11}=\frac{\det(R_1^\star)}{\det(R_1)}.\]
\[R_1=\begin{pmatrix}1\end{pmatrix}\]
\[R_1^\star=\begin{pmatrix}-\frac15\end{pmatrix}.\]
\[\det(R_1)=1.\]
\[\det(R_1^\star)=-\frac15.\]
\[a_{11}=\frac{-1/5}{1}=-\frac15.\]
\[\boxed{\operatorname{PACF}(1)=\alpha_X(1)=-\frac15.}\]

\(h=2\)

\[R_2a_2=\rho_2.\]
\[a_2=\begin{pmatrix}a_{21}\\a_{22}\end{pmatrix}\]
\[\rho_2=\begin{pmatrix}\rho_X(1)\\\rho_X(2)\end{pmatrix}.\]

We want the last coefficient: \(\operatorname{PACF}(2)=\alpha_X(2)=a_{22}\). Insert the ACF values into the system:

\[\begin{pmatrix}1&-\frac15\\-\frac15&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a_{21}\\a_{22}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}-\frac15\\\frac15\end{pmatrix}.\]

Use Cramer’s rule for \(a_{22}\): replace the second / last column of \(R_2\) by \(\rho_2\) to form \(R_2^\star\).

\[a_{22}=\frac{\det(R_2^\star)}{\det(R_2)}.\]
\[R_2=\begin{pmatrix}1&-\frac15\\-\frac15&1\end{pmatrix}\]
\[R_2^\star=\begin{pmatrix}1&-\frac15\\-\frac15&\frac15\end{pmatrix}.\]
\[\det(R_2)=1\cdot1-\left(-\frac15\right)\left(-\frac15\right)=\frac{24}{25}.\]
\[\det(R_2^\star)=1\cdot\frac15-\left(-\frac15\right)\left(-\frac15\right)=\frac4{25}.\]
\[a_{22}=\frac{4/25}{24/25}=\frac16.\]
\[\boxed{\operatorname{PACF}(2)=\alpha_X(2)=\frac16.}\]

\(h>2\)

\[\boxed{\operatorname{PACF}(h)=\alpha_X(h)=0,\qquad h>2.}\]

AR(2): PACF is zero for every lag greater than 2.

An AR(2) process depends directly only on its first two lags. Once \(X_{t-1}\) and \(X_{t-2}\) are controlled for, older lags provide no additional direct linear dependence. Therefore the PACF is zero for every lag greater than 2; in particular, \(\alpha_X(3)=0\).

AR(2) ACF and PACF up to lag 3
Lag \(h\)0123
ACF \(\rho_X(h)\)\(1\)\(-\frac15\)\(\frac15\)\(-\frac1{15}\)
PACF \(\alpha_X(h)\)—\(-\frac15\)\(\frac16\)\(0\)
Exercise 2

An MA(2) process

Consider

\[Y_t=1+12\varepsilon_t-7\varepsilon_{t-1}+\varepsilon_{t-2},\]

where \(\varepsilon_t\) is a weak white noise with variance \(\sigma_\varepsilon^2\).

  1. Give the mean of \(Y_t\), and write the demeaned process \(\widetilde Y_t\).
  2. Show that the equation is invertible and give its AR\((\infty)\) representation.
  3. Compute the ACF up to \(h=3\).
  4. Compute the PACF up to \(h=3\).

Given information & notation

\[E(\varepsilon_t)=0,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon_t)=\sigma_\varepsilon^2,\qquad \operatorname{Cov}(\varepsilon_t,\varepsilon_{t-h})=0\ (h\ne0).\]

Write \(\widetilde Y_t=Y_t-\mu_Y\).

Question 1 Mean and demeaned process

What to find · Formula

Use the zero mean of the innovations, then centre the process around its mean.

\[\mu_Y=E(Y_t),\qquad \widetilde Y_t=Y_t-\mu_Y.\]
\[\mu_Y=E(Y_t)=1+12E(\varepsilon_t)-7E(\varepsilon_{t-1})+E(\varepsilon_{t-2})=1.\]
\[\boxed{\mu_Y=1.}\]

Demeaned process

We know that \(\mu_Y=1\). Subtract \(1\) directly from both sides of the model and write \(\widetilde Y_t=Y_t-1\):

\[\boxed{\widetilde Y_t=12\varepsilon_t-7\varepsilon_{t-1}+\varepsilon_{t-2}.}\]

Why divide by 12?

By convention, an MA(\(q\)) is written with coefficient \(1\) on the current innovation \(\varepsilon_t\):

\[X_t=\varepsilon_t+\theta_1\varepsilon_{t-1}+\cdots+\theta_q\varepsilon_{t-q}.\]

In the demeaned equation above, the coefficient on \(\varepsilon_t\) is \(12\), not \(1\). We therefore divide the whole equation by \(12\):

\[\boxed{\frac{\widetilde Y_t}{12}=\varepsilon_t-\frac7{12}\varepsilon_{t-1}+\frac1{12}\varepsilon_{t-2}.}\]

Hence the normalized MA polynomial is

\[\boxed{\Theta(L)=1-\frac7{12}L+\frac1{12}L^2.}\]

Question 2 Invertibility and AR\((\infty)\)

What to find · Formula

Calculate the inverse roots \(r_1,r_2\), then use them in the factors \((1-r_1L)(1-r_2L)\). Inverse roots inside the unit circle make this MA(2) invertible.

\[\frac1{12}\widetilde Y_t=\Theta(L)\varepsilon_t,\qquad r^2\Theta(r^{-1})=0,\qquad |r_i|<1.\]
\[\frac1{12}\widetilde Y_t=\Theta(L)\varepsilon_t,\qquad \Theta(L)=1-\frac7{12}L+\frac1{12}L^2.\]

Inverse roots

Replace \(L\) by \(r^{-1}\), then multiply by \(r^2\) to remove the negative powers:

\[r^2\Theta(r^{-1})=0\] \[r^2\left(1-\frac7{12}r^{-1}+\frac1{12}r^{-2}\right)=0\] \[r^2-\frac7{12}r+\frac1{12}=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad12r^2-7r+1=0.\]

Use the quadratic formula:

\[\Delta=(-7)^2-4(12)(1)=1,\qquad r=\frac{7\pm\sqrt{1}}{24}.\] \[r_1=\frac{7+1}{24}=\frac13,\qquad r_2=\frac{7-1}{24}=\frac14.\]
\[\boxed{r_1=\frac13,\qquad r_2=\frac14.}\] \[|r_1|=\frac13<1,\qquad |r_2|=\frac14<1.\]

Both inverse roots lie inside the unit circle, so the MA(2) is invertible.

Factorization and partial fractions

Use the inverse roots in \((1-r_1L)(1-r_2L)\), then find the partial-fraction coefficients:

\[\Theta(L)=(1-r_1L)(1-r_2L)\] \[=\left(1-\frac13L\right)\left(1-\frac14L\right),\] \[\frac{1}{(1-\frac13L)(1-\frac14L)}=\frac{C_1}{1-\frac13L}+\frac{C_2}{1-\frac14L}.\]

Multiply by the common denominator, then group constants and coefficients of \(L\):

\[\begin{aligned}1&=C_1\left(1-\frac14L\right)+C_2\left(1-\frac13L\right)\\&=(C_1+C_2)+\left(-\frac{C_1}{4}-\frac{C_2}{3}\right)L.\end{aligned}\]

The left side is \(1+0L\), so

\[\begin{cases}C_1+C_2=1,\\-\frac{C_1}{4}-\frac{C_2}{3}=0.\end{cases}\qquad C_2=-\frac34C_1\quad\Rightarrow\quad\frac14C_1=1.\] \[\boxed{C_1=4,\qquad C_2=-3.}\] \[\Theta(L)^{-1}=4\left(1-\frac13L\right)^{-1}-3\left(1-\frac14L\right)^{-1}.\]
\[\Theta(L)^{-1}=4\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac13\right)^jL^j-3\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac14\right)^jL^j.\]

Apply the inverse polynomial to \(\frac1{12}\widetilde Y_t\), then separate the \(j=0\) term:

\[\varepsilon_t=\frac1{12}\widetilde Y_t+\frac1{12}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left[4\left(\frac13\right)^j-3\left(\frac14\right)^j\right]\widetilde Y_{t-j}.\]

Multiply by 12 and move the terms with \(j\geq1\) to the other side:

\[\boxed{\widetilde Y_t=12\varepsilon_t-\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left[4\left(\frac13\right)^j-3\left(\frac14\right)^j\right]\widetilde Y_{t-j}.}\]

Question 3 ACF up to lag 3

What to find · Formula

Compute \(\gamma_Y(h)=E(\widetilde Y_t\widetilde Y_{t-h})\), then divide by \(\gamma_Y(0)\).

\[\rho_Y(h)=\frac{\gamma_Y(h)}{\gamma_Y(0)}.\]

At lag zero, all cross-date products vanish:

\[\gamma_Y(0)=E\!\left[(12\varepsilon_t-7\varepsilon_{t-1}+\varepsilon_{t-2})^2\right]\]\[=(12^2+(-7)^2+1^2)\sigma_\varepsilon^2=194\sigma_\varepsilon^2.\]

At lag one, the common innovation dates are \(t-1\) and \(t-2\):

\[\widetilde Y_{t-1}=12\varepsilon_{t-1}-7\varepsilon_{t-2}+\varepsilon_{t-3},\]\[\gamma_Y(1)=(-7)(12)\sigma_\varepsilon^2+(1)(-7)\sigma_\varepsilon^2=-91\sigma_\varepsilon^2.\]

At lag two, only the innovation dated \(t-2\) is common. At lag three, there is no common innovation date:

\[\gamma_Y(2)=(1)(12)\sigma_\varepsilon^2=12\sigma_\varepsilon^2,\qquad\gamma_Y(3)=0.\]
MA(2) ACF up to lag 3
Lag \(h\)0123
\(\rho_Y(h)\)\(1\)\(-\frac{91}{194}\)\(\frac6{97}\)\(0\)

\(-\frac{91}{194}\simeq-0.4691\) and \(\frac6{97}\simeq0.0619\).

The ACF of this MA(2) is zero for every lag greater than 2: \(\rho_Y(h)=0\) for \(h>2\).

Question 4 PACF up to lag 3

\(h=1\)

\[R_1a_1=\rho_1.\]
\[a_1=\begin{pmatrix}a_{11}\end{pmatrix}\]
\[\rho_1=\begin{pmatrix}\rho_Y(1)\end{pmatrix}.\]

We want the last coefficient: \(\operatorname{PACF}(1)=\alpha_Y(1)=a_{11}\). Insert the ACF values into the system:

\[\begin{pmatrix}1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a_{11}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}-\frac{91}{194}\end{pmatrix}.\]

Use Cramer’s rule for \(a_{11}\): replace the first (and last) column of \(R_1\) by \(\rho_1\) to form \(R_1^\star\).

\[a_{11}=\frac{\det(R_1^\star)}{\det(R_1)}.\]
\[R_1=\begin{pmatrix}1\end{pmatrix}\]
\[R_1^\star=\begin{pmatrix}-\frac{91}{194}\end{pmatrix}.\]
\[\det(R_1)=1.\]
\[\det(R_1^\star)=-\frac{91}{194}.\]
\[a_{11}=\frac{-91/194}{1}=-\frac{91}{194}\simeq-0.4691.\]
\[\boxed{\operatorname{PACF}(1)=\alpha_Y(1)=-\frac{91}{194}\simeq-0.4691.}\]

\(h=2\)

\[R_2a_2=\rho_2.\]
\[a_2=\begin{pmatrix}a_{21}\\a_{22}\end{pmatrix}\]
\[\rho_2=\begin{pmatrix}\rho_Y(1)\\\rho_Y(2)\end{pmatrix}.\]

We want the last coefficient: \(\operatorname{PACF}(2)=\alpha_Y(2)=a_{22}\). Insert the ACF values into the system:

\[\begin{pmatrix}1&-\frac{91}{194}\\-\frac{91}{194}&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a_{21}\\a_{22}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}-\frac{91}{194}\\\frac6{97}\end{pmatrix}.\]

Use Cramer’s rule for \(a_{22}\): replace the second / last column of \(R_2\) by \(\rho_2\) to form \(R_2^\star\).

\[a_{22}=\frac{\det(R_2^\star)}{\det(R_2)}.\]
\[R_2=\begin{pmatrix}1&-\frac{91}{194}\\-\frac{91}{194}&1\end{pmatrix}\]
\[R_2^\star=\begin{pmatrix}1&-\frac{91}{194}\\-\frac{91}{194}&\frac6{97}\end{pmatrix}.\]
\[\det(R_2)=1\cdot1-\left(-\frac{91}{194}\right)^2=\frac{29355}{37636}.\]
\[\det(R_2^\star)=1\cdot\frac6{97}-\left(-\frac{91}{194}\right)^2=-\frac{5953}{37636}.\]
\[a_{22}=\frac{-5953/37636}{29355/37636}=-\frac{5953}{29355}\simeq-0.2028.\]
\[\boxed{\operatorname{PACF}(2)=\alpha_Y(2)=-\frac{5953}{29355}\simeq-0.2028.}\]

\(h=3\)

For an MA(2), \(\rho_Y(h)=0\) for \(h>2\), so \(\rho_Y(3)=0\). But PACF(3) is not necessarily zero.

PACF(3) is \(a_{33}\), obtained after controlling for lags 1 and 2. Even though \(\rho_Y(3)=0\), the partial autocorrelation can be nonzero because \(\rho_Y(1)\) and \(\rho_Y(2)\) enter the Yule–Walker system.

\[R_3a_3=\rho_3.\]
\[a_3=\begin{pmatrix}a_{31}\\a_{32}\\a_{33}\end{pmatrix}\]
\[\rho_3=\begin{pmatrix}\rho_Y(1)\\\rho_Y(2)\\0\end{pmatrix}.\]

We want the last coefficient: \(\operatorname{PACF}(3)=\alpha_Y(3)=a_{33}\). Insert the ACF values into the system:

\[\begin{pmatrix}1&-\frac{91}{194}&\frac6{97}\\-\frac{91}{194}&1&-\frac{91}{194}\\\frac6{97}&-\frac{91}{194}&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a_{31}\\a_{32}\\a_{33}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}-\frac{91}{194}\\\frac6{97}\\0\end{pmatrix}.\]

Use Cramer’s rule for \(a_{33}\): replace the third / last column of \(R_3\) by \(\rho_3\) to form \(R_3^\star\).

\[a_{33}=\frac{\det(R_3^\star)}{\det(R_3)}.\]
\[R_3=\begin{pmatrix}1&-\frac{91}{194}&\frac6{97}\\-\frac{91}{194}&1&-\frac{91}{194}\\\frac6{97}&-\frac{91}{194}&1\end{pmatrix}\]
\[R_3^\star=\begin{pmatrix}1&-\frac{91}{194}&-\frac{91}{194}\\-\frac{91}{194}&1&\frac6{97}\\\frac6{97}&-\frac{91}{194}&0\end{pmatrix}.\]

Expand each determinant along the first row, with signs \(+,-,+\):

\[\begin{aligned}\det(R_3)&=1\left[1\cdot1-\left(-\frac{91}{194}\right)^2\right]\\&\quad-\left(-\frac{91}{194}\right)\left[\left(-\frac{91}{194}\right)\cdot1-\left(-\frac{91}{194}\right)\frac6{97}\right]\\&\quad+\frac6{97}\left[\left(-\frac{91}{194}\right)^2-1\cdot\frac6{97}\right]\\&=\frac{1064791}{1825346}.\end{aligned}\]
\[\begin{aligned}\det(R_3^\star)&=1\left[1\cdot0-\frac6{97}\left(-\frac{91}{194}\right)\right]\\&\quad-\left(-\frac{91}{194}\right)\left[\left(-\frac{91}{194}\right)\cdot0-\frac6{97}\cdot\frac6{97}\right]\\&\quad+\left(-\frac{91}{194}\right)\left[\left(-\frac{91}{194}\right)^2-1\cdot\frac6{97}\right]\\&=-\frac{342979}{7301384}.\end{aligned}\]
\[a_{33}=\frac{-342979/7301384}{1064791/1825346}=-\frac{3769}{46804}\simeq-0.0805.\]
\[\boxed{\operatorname{PACF}(3)=\alpha_Y(3)=-\frac{3769}{46804}\simeq-0.0805.}\]
