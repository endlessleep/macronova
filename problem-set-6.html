PROBLEM SET 6

Exercise 1

Main outputs of a VAR process

Consider the structural VAR(1), using its causal stationary solution:

\[X_t=\Phi X_{t-1}+S\epsilon_t,\]
\[\Phi=\begin{pmatrix}0.7&0\\0&0.3\end{pmatrix},\qquad S=\begin{pmatrix}0.1&0\\0.1&0.2\end{pmatrix},\]

where \(\epsilon_t\overset{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}\mathcal N(0,I_2)\) are the structural shocks.

  1. Check the stability of the VAR.
  2. Compute the VMA(\(\infty\)) form of \(X_t\).
  3. Compute the orthogonalised IRFs.
  4. Compute the FEVD at horizons \(h=1\) and \(h=2\).
  5. Compute \(\Gamma_X(h)=\operatorname{Cov}(X_t,X_{t-h})\), the autocovariance function of \(X_t\).
  6. Compute the FEVD at horizon \(h\to\infty\).

Question 1Stability of the VAR

Formulas to use — Question 1

Eigenvalues: solve the characteristic equation, then check their moduli.

\[\det(\lambda I_n-\Phi)=0,\qquad\rho(\Phi)=\max_i|\lambda_i|<1.\]

Solution

\[\begin{aligned}\det(\lambda I_2-\Phi)&=(\lambda-0.7)(\lambda-0.3)\\&=\lambda^2-\lambda+0.21.\end{aligned}\]

The eigenvalues are \(0.7\) and \(0.3\). Both lie inside the unit circle:

\[\boxed{\rho(\Phi)=0.7<1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\text{the VAR is stable.}}\]

Question 2VMA(∞) representation

Formulas to use — Question 2

Matrix powers: diagonalization raises only the eigenvalues to the power j.

\[\Phi=D\Lambda D^{-1}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\Phi^j=D\Lambda^jD^{-1}.\]

Stable structural VAR(1): iterate backward and collect past shocks.

\[X_t=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\Phi^jS\epsilon_{t-j},\qquad\Theta_j=\Phi^jS.\]

Solution

\(\Phi\) is already diagonal. Its eigenvectors are \((1,0)'\) and \((0,1)'\), so \(D=D^{-1}=I_2\):

\[\Phi^j=\begin{pmatrix}0.7^j&0\\0&0.3^j\end{pmatrix}.\]

Substitute the previous observation into the model:

\[\begin{aligned}X_t&=\Phi(\Phi X_{t-2}+S\epsilon_{t-1})+S\epsilon_t\\&=\Phi^2X_{t-2}+\Phi S\epsilon_{t-1}+S\epsilon_t.\end{aligned}\]

Repeated substitution and stability give

\[\boxed{X_t=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\Phi^jS\epsilon_{t-j}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\Theta_j\epsilon_{t-j}.}\]
\[\Theta_j=\Phi^jS=\begin{pmatrix}0.1(0.7)^j&0\\0.1(0.3)^j&0.2(0.3)^j\end{pmatrix}.\]

Question 3Orthogonalised impulse responses

Formulas to use — Question 3

Innovation covariance and structural responses: under unit-variance shocks,

\[\varepsilon_t=S\epsilon_t,\qquad\Sigma_\varepsilon=SS',\qquad\operatorname{IRF}(h)=\Theta_h=\Phi^hS.\]

Cumulative responses: sum each coefficient sequence, including the impact period.

\[\sum_{h=0}^{\infty}r^h=\frac1{1-r},\qquad |r|<1.\]

Solution

The reduced-form innovation is a combination of the structural shocks:

\[\begin{aligned}\Sigma_\varepsilon=SS'&=\begin{pmatrix}0.1&0\\0.1&0.2\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0.1&0.1\\0&0.2\end{pmatrix}\\&=\begin{pmatrix}0.01&0.01\\0.01&0.05\end{pmatrix}.\end{aligned}\]

The structural shocks are already orthogonal with variance 1. Use the VMA coefficients from Question 2:

\[\boxed{\Theta_h=\operatorname{IRF}(h)=\begin{pmatrix}0.1(0.7)^h&0\\0.1(0.3)^h&0.2(0.3)^h\end{pmatrix},\quad h\ge0.}\]

Column \(j\) gives the responses to a unit, hence one-standard-deviation, shock \(\epsilon_j\). At impact, \(\Theta_0=S\).

\[\sum_{h=0}^{\infty}\Theta_h=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{0.1}{1-0.7}&0\\\frac{0.1}{1-0.3}&\frac{0.2}{1-0.3}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac13&0\\\frac17&\frac27\end{pmatrix}.\]

Question 4FEVD at horizons 1 and 2

Formulas to use — Question 4

Unit-variance orthogonal shocks: square the responses and add over the forecast-error horizons.

\[\operatorname{FEVD}(h)\big|_{\epsilon_j}^{x_i}=\frac{\sum_{k=0}^{h-1}(\theta_{ij}^{(k)})^2}{\sum_{\ell=1}^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{h-1}(\theta_{i\ell}^{(k)})^2}.\]

The numerator is shock \(j\)’s contribution; the denominator adds both shocks. Use \(\Theta_0\) at \(h=1\), and \(\Theta_0,\Theta_1\) at \(h=2\). Rows identify variables and columns identify shocks.

Solution

Horizon h = 1

\[\Theta_0=S=\begin{pmatrix}0.1&0\\0.1&0.2\end{pmatrix}.\]
\[\begin{aligned}\operatorname{FEVD}(1)\big|_{\epsilon_1}^{x_1}&=\frac{0.1^2}{0.1^2+0^2}=100\%,\\\operatorname{FEVD}(1)\big|_{\epsilon_2}^{x_1}&=\frac{0^2}{0.1^2+0^2}=0\%,\\\operatorname{FEVD}(1)\big|_{\epsilon_1}^{x_2}&=\frac{0.1^2}{0.1^2+0.2^2}=20\%,\\\operatorname{FEVD}(1)\big|_{\epsilon_2}^{x_2}&=\frac{0.2^2}{0.1^2+0.2^2}=80\%.\end{aligned}\]
\[\boxed{\operatorname{FEVD}(1)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0.2&0.8\end{pmatrix}.}\]

Horizon h = 2

Include the impact responses and the responses one period later:

\[\Theta_1=\Phi S=\begin{pmatrix}0.07&0\\0.03&0.06\end{pmatrix}.\]
\[\begin{aligned}\operatorname{FEVD}(2)\big|_{\epsilon_1}^{x_1}&=\frac{0.1^2+0.07^2}{0.1^2+0.07^2+0^2+0^2}=100\%,\\\operatorname{FEVD}(2)\big|_{\epsilon_2}^{x_1}&=0\%.\end{aligned}\]
\[\begin{aligned}\operatorname{FEVD}(2)\big|_{\epsilon_1}^{x_2}&=\frac{0.1^2+0.03^2}{0.1^2+0.03^2+0.2^2+0.06^2}\\&=\frac{0.0109}{0.0545}=20\%,\\\operatorname{FEVD}(2)\big|_{\epsilon_2}^{x_2}&=\frac{0.2^2+0.06^2}{0.1^2+0.03^2+0.2^2+0.06^2}\\&=\frac{0.0436}{0.0545}=80\%.\end{aligned}\]
\[\boxed{\operatorname{FEVD}(2)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0.2&0.8\end{pmatrix}.}\]

Question 5Autocovariance function

Formulas to use — Question 5

Start with the unconditional covariance: current innovations are uncorrelated with past observations.

\[\Gamma_X(0)=\Phi\Gamma_X(0)\Phi'+\Sigma_\varepsilon.\]

Vectorize to solve: vec stacks columns, and the Kronecker product replaces each entry of its first matrix by that entry times its second matrix.

\[\operatorname{vec}(ABC)=(C'\otimes A)\operatorname{vec}(B).\]
\[\operatorname{vec}(\Gamma_X(0))=(I_4-\Phi\otimes\Phi)^{-1}\operatorname{vec}(\Sigma_\varepsilon).\]

Propagate to other lags:

\[\Gamma_X(h)=\Phi^h\Gamma_X(0),\quad h\ge1,\qquad\Gamma_X(-h)=\Gamma_X(h)'.\]

Solution

Unconditional covariance

Taking the covariance of the VAR gives the Lyapunov equation. Apply the column-stacking identity:

\[\Gamma_X(0)=\Phi\Gamma_X(0)\Phi'+\Sigma_\varepsilon,\]
\[\begin{aligned}\operatorname{vec}(\Gamma_X(0))&=(\Phi\otimes\Phi)\operatorname{vec}(\Gamma_X(0))\\&\quad+\operatorname{vec}(\Sigma_\varepsilon).\end{aligned}\]

Move the first term to the left and invert \(I_4-\Phi\otimes\Phi\). Since \(\Phi\) is diagonal,

\[\Phi\otimes\Phi=\begin{pmatrix}0.49&0&0&0\\0&0.21&0&0\\0&0&0.21&0\\0&0&0&0.09\end{pmatrix}.\]
\[\begin{aligned}\operatorname{vec}(\Gamma_X(0))&=\begin{pmatrix}1/0.51&0&0&0\\0&1/0.79&0&0\\0&0&1/0.79&0\\0&0&0&1/0.91\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0.01\\0.01\\0.01\\0.05\end{pmatrix}\\&=\begin{pmatrix}1/51\\1/79\\1/79\\5/91\end{pmatrix}.\end{aligned}\]

Unstack column by column: the first two entries form column 1, and the last two form column 2.

\[\boxed{\Gamma_X(0)=\begin{pmatrix}\frac1{51}&\frac1{79}\\\frac1{79}&\frac5{91}\end{pmatrix}\approx\begin{pmatrix}0.019608&0.012658\\0.012658&0.054945\end{pmatrix}.}\]

Covariance at lag h

For positive lags, the current innovation contributes no covariance with the past:

\[\Gamma_X(h)=\Phi\Gamma_X(h-1)=\Phi^h\Gamma_X(0).\]
\[\boxed{\Gamma_X(h)=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{0.7^h}{51}&\frac{0.7^h}{79}\\\frac{0.3^h}{79}&\frac{5(0.3)^h}{91}\end{pmatrix},\quad h\ge0.}\]
\[\Gamma_X(1)\approx\begin{pmatrix}0.013725&0.008861\\0.003797&0.016484\end{pmatrix},\]
\[\Gamma_X(2)\approx\begin{pmatrix}0.009608&0.006203\\0.001139&0.004945\end{pmatrix}.\]

For negative lags, \(\Gamma_X(-h)=\Gamma_X(h)'\). As \(h\to\infty\), both powers tend to zero, so \(\Gamma_X(h)\to0\).

Question 6Long-horizon FEVD

Formulas to use — Question 6

Sum the squared responses: use the geometric-series limit in the numerator and denominator of each variance share.

\[\sum_{k=0}^{h-1}r^k=\frac{1-r^h}{1-r}\longrightarrow\frac1{1-r},\qquad |r|<1.\]
\[\operatorname{FEVD}(\infty)\big|_{\epsilon_j}^{x_i}=\frac{\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(\theta_{ij}^{(k)})^2}{[\Gamma_X(0)]_{ii}}.\]

For a stable VAR, \(\Sigma_e(h)\to\Gamma_X(0)\): long-horizon forecast uncertainty reaches the unconditional covariance.

Solution

For \(x_1\), only shock 1 contributes:

\[\operatorname{FEVD}(\infty)\big|_{\epsilon_1}^{x_1}=\frac{0.01/(1-0.7^2)}{0.01/(1-0.7^2)}=100\%,\]
\[\operatorname{FEVD}(\infty)\big|_{\epsilon_2}^{x_1}=0\%.\]

For \(x_2\), the two squared responses share the same decay factor \(0.3^{2k}\):

\[\operatorname{FEVD}(\infty)\big|_{\epsilon_1}^{x_2}=\frac{0.01/(1-0.3^2)}{0.05/(1-0.3^2)}=\frac15,\]
\[\operatorname{FEVD}(\infty)\big|_{\epsilon_2}^{x_2}=\frac{0.04/(1-0.3^2)}{0.05/(1-0.3^2)}=\frac45.\]
\[\boxed{\operatorname{FEVD}(\infty)=\begin{pmatrix}100\%&0\%\\20\%&80\%\end{pmatrix}.}\]

The shares are constant at every horizon in this process: both responses of \(x_2\) decay at the same rate \(0.3^h\), which cancels in their variance shares.
