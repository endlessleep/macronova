Exercise 1

Training

Let \(X_1,X_2,Y_1,Y_2\) be real-valued random variables and \(a,b,c,d\) four real numbers. Show that:

  1. \[V(aX_1+b)=a^2V(X_1).\]
  2. \(\operatorname{Cov}(aX_1+b,cY_1+d)\) \(=ac\operatorname{Cov}(X_1,Y_1).\)
  3. \(\operatorname{Cov}(aX_1+bX_2,cY_1+dY_2)\) \(=ac\operatorname{Cov}(X_1,Y_1)\) \({}+ad\operatorname{Cov}(X_1,Y_2)\) \({}+bc\operatorname{Cov}(X_2,Y_1)\) \({}+bd\operatorname{Cov}(X_2,Y_2).\)
FORMULA
\[V(X)=E(X^2)-[E(X)]^2\]
\(\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)\) \(=E(XY)-E(X)E(Y)\)

The variances and covariances below are understood to exist; in particular, the variables have finite second moments.

Step 1i) Variance of an affine transformation

Apply the variance formula, then expand both terms:

\(V(aX_1+b)=E[(aX_1+b)^2]\) \({}-[E(aX_1+b)]^2.\)
\[E[(aX_1+b)^2]\]\[=a^2E(X_1^2)+2abE(X_1)+b^2\]\[[E(aX_1+b)]^2\]\[=a^2[E(X_1)]^2+2abE(X_1)+b^2.\]

The terms \(2abE(X_1)\) and \(b^2\) cancel:

\[V(aX_1+b)=a^2E(X_1^2)-a^2[E(X_1)]^2\]\[=a^2\bigl(E(X_1^2)-[E(X_1)]^2\bigr).\]
\[\boxed{V(aX_1+b)=a^2V(X_1)}\]

Step 2ii) Covariance of affine transformations

Apply the covariance formula:

\(\operatorname{Cov}(aX_1+b,cY_1+d)\) \(=E[(aX_1+b)(cY_1+d)]\) \({}-E(aX_1+b)E(cY_1+d).\)

Expand the expected product and the product of expectations separately:

\[E[(aX_1+b)(cY_1+d)]\]
\(=acE(X_1Y_1)+adE(X_1)\) \({}+bcE(Y_1)+bd.\)
\[E(aX_1+b)E(cY_1+d)\]
\(=acE(X_1)E(Y_1)+adE(X_1)\) \({}+bcE(Y_1)+bd.\)

Subtract: all terms involving the constant shifts cancel.

\[\operatorname{Cov}(aX_1+b,cY_1+d)\]\[=ac\bigl[E(X_1Y_1)-E(X_1)E(Y_1)\bigr].\]
\(\operatorname{Cov}(aX_1+b,cY_1+d)\) \(=ac\operatorname{Cov}(X_1,Y_1).\)

Step 3iii) Covariance of two linear combinations

Use the same definition and expand the four products:

\(\operatorname{Cov}(aX_1+bX_2,cY_1+dY_2)\) \(=E[(aX_1+bX_2)(cY_1+dY_2)]\) \({}-E(aX_1+bX_2)E(cY_1+dY_2).\)
\[E[(aX_1+bX_2)(cY_1+dY_2)]\]
\(=acE(X_1Y_1)+adE(X_1Y_2)\) \({}+bcE(X_2Y_1)+bdE(X_2Y_2).\)
\[E(aX_1+bX_2)E(cY_1+dY_2)\]
\(=acE(X_1)E(Y_1)+adE(X_1)E(Y_2)\) \({}+bcE(X_2)E(Y_1)+bdE(X_2)E(Y_2).\)

Subtract and group each expected product with its matching product of expectations:

\[\operatorname{Cov}(aX_1+bX_2,cY_1+dY_2)\]\[=ac[E(X_1Y_1)-E(X_1)E(Y_1)]\]\[\quad{}+ad[E(X_1Y_2)-E(X_1)E(Y_2)]\]\[\quad{}+bc[E(X_2Y_1)-E(X_2)E(Y_1)]\]\[\quad{}+bd[E(X_2Y_2)-E(X_2)E(Y_2)].\]
\(\operatorname{Cov}(aX_1+bX_2,cY_1+dY_2)\) \(=ac\operatorname{Cov}(X_1,Y_1)\) \({}+ad\operatorname{Cov}(X_1,Y_2)\) \({}+bc\operatorname{Cov}(X_2,Y_1)\) \({}+bd\operatorname{Cov}(X_2,Y_2).\)
Exercise 2

Stationarity

Let \((\varepsilon_t)_{t\in\mathbb Z}\) be a weak white noise with variance \(\sigma^2\). For every \(t\) and every \(h\ne0\),

\[E(\varepsilon_t)=0,\]\[V(\varepsilon_t)=E(\varepsilon_t^2)=\sigma^2<\infty,\]\[\operatorname{Cov}(\varepsilon_t,\varepsilon_{t-h})\]\[=E(\varepsilon_t\varepsilon_{t-h})=0.\]

Are the following processes second-order stationary?

  1. \[Y_t=\varepsilon_t-\varepsilon_{t-1}.\]
  2. \[Z_t=a\varepsilon_t+b\varepsilon_{t-1}.\]
  3. \(W_0=0,\) \(W_t=W_{t-1}+\varepsilon_t\quad(t>0).\)
COURSE REMINDER

Check that the mean and variance are finite and constant, and that the covariance depends only on the lag:

\(E(X_t)=\mu,\) \(V(X_t)=\gamma_X(0)<\infty,\)
\[\operatorname{Cov}(X_t,X_{t-h})=\gamma_X(h).\]

For white noise, only products with matching dates have nonzero expectation. Negative lags follow by covariance symmetry.

Step 1\(Y_t=\varepsilon_t-\varepsilon_{t-1}\)

First compute the mean and variance:

\[E(Y_t)=E(\varepsilon_t)-E(\varepsilon_{t-1})=0.\]
\(V(Y_t)=V(\varepsilon_t)+V(\varepsilon_{t-1})\) \({}-2\operatorname{Cov}(\varepsilon_t,\varepsilon_{t-1})\)
\[=\sigma^2+\sigma^2-0=2\sigma^2.\]

At lag 1, the two observations share one innovation:

\[\operatorname{Cov}(Y_t,Y_{t-1})\]\[=E[(\varepsilon_t-\varepsilon_{t-1})(\varepsilon_{t-1}-\varepsilon_{t-2})]\]
\(=E(\varepsilon_t\varepsilon_{t-1})\) \({}-E(\varepsilon_t\varepsilon_{t-2})\) \({}-E(\varepsilon_{t-1}^2)\) \({}+E(\varepsilon_{t-1}\varepsilon_{t-2})\)
\[=0-0-\sigma^2+0=-\sigma^2.\]

For \(h>1\), expand in the same way. All dates are distinct in each product:

\[\operatorname{Cov}(Y_t,Y_{t-h})\]
\(=E(\varepsilon_t\varepsilon_{t-h})\) \({}-E(\varepsilon_t\varepsilon_{t-h-1})\) \({}-E(\varepsilon_{t-1}\varepsilon_{t-h})\) \({}+E(\varepsilon_{t-1}\varepsilon_{t-h-1})\)
\[=0.\]
\[\gamma_Y(h)=\begin{cases}2\sigma^2, & h=0,\\[3pt]-\sigma^2, & |h|=1,\\[3pt]0, & |h|>1.\end{cases}\]

\(Y_t\) is second-order stationary. Its mean and variance are constant, and its covariance depends only on \(h\).

Step 2\(Z_t=a\varepsilon_t+b\varepsilon_{t-1}\)

\[E(Z_t)=aE(\varepsilon_t)+bE(\varepsilon_{t-1})=0.\]
\(V(Z_t)=a^2V(\varepsilon_t)+b^2V(\varepsilon_{t-1})\) \({}+2ab\operatorname{Cov}(\varepsilon_t,\varepsilon_{t-1})\)
\[=(a^2+b^2)\sigma^2.\]

At lag 1, keep the term whose innovation dates coincide:

\[\operatorname{Cov}(Z_t,Z_{t-1})\]\[=E[(a\varepsilon_t+b\varepsilon_{t-1})(a\varepsilon_{t-1}+b\varepsilon_{t-2})]\]
\(=a^2E(\varepsilon_t\varepsilon_{t-1})\) \({}+abE(\varepsilon_t\varepsilon_{t-2})\) \({}+abE(\varepsilon_{t-1}^2)\) \({}+b^2E(\varepsilon_{t-1}\varepsilon_{t-2})\)
\[=ab\sigma^2.\]

For \(h>1\), all four expectations are zero:

\[\operatorname{Cov}(Z_t,Z_{t-h})\]
\(=a^2E(\varepsilon_t\varepsilon_{t-h})\) \({}+abE(\varepsilon_t\varepsilon_{t-h-1})\) \({}+abE(\varepsilon_{t-1}\varepsilon_{t-h})\) \({}+b^2E(\varepsilon_{t-1}\varepsilon_{t-h-1})\)
\[=0.\]
\[\gamma_Z(h)=\begin{cases}(a^2+b^2)\sigma^2, & h=0,\\[3pt]ab\sigma^2, & |h|=1,\\[3pt]0, & |h|>1.\end{cases}\]

\(Z_t\) is second-order stationary. All three conditions hold.

Step 3\(W_t=W_{t-1}+\varepsilon_t\)

Unroll the recursion, starting from the fixed value 0:

\[W_1=\varepsilon_1,\]\[W_2=\varepsilon_1+\varepsilon_2,\]\[W_3=\varepsilon_1+\varepsilon_2+\varepsilon_3,\]\[W_t=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\varepsilon_i.\]

The innovations have zero mean and are uncorrelated across dates:

\[E(W_t)=\sum_{i=1}^{t}E(\varepsilon_i)=0,\]\[V(W_t)=\sum_{i=1}^{t}V(\varepsilon_i)=t\sigma^2.\]

For \(\sigma^2>0\), \(W_t\) is not second-order stationary: its variance increases with \(t\). A constant mean alone is not enough.

If \(\sigma^2=0\), the process is identically zero; the non-stationarity conclusion concerns nonzero innovation variance.

Exercise 3

Transformations of stationary processes

2025/2026

  1. Let \((X_t)_{t\in\mathbb Z}\) and \((Y_t)_{t\in\mathbb Z}\) be second-order stationary, with \(E(X_t)=E(Y_t)=0\), and let \(Z_t=X_t+Y_t\). Is \(Z_t\) necessarily second-order stationary? Show that it is if the two processes are uncorrelated.

  2. Let \((\varepsilon_t)_{t\in\mathbb Z}\) be a weak white noise with variance \(\sigma^2\). Fix \(\mu\in\mathbb R\) and coefficients \((\theta_k)\) such that

    \[\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\theta_k^2<\infty.\]

    Define

    \[U_t=\mu+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\theta_k\varepsilon_{t-k}.\]

    Compute the expectation, variance and covariance \(\gamma_U(h)=\operatorname{Cov}(U_t,U_{t-h})\), for \(h>0\).

COURSE REMINDER

As in Exercise 2, check the mean, variance and covariance as functions of time and lag.

\(V(X+Y)=V(X)+V(Y)\) \({}+2\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)\)
\(\operatorname{Cov}(X_t+Y_t,X_{t-h}+Y_{t-h})\) \(=\operatorname{Cov}(X_t,X_{t-h})\) \({}+\operatorname{Cov}(X_t,Y_{t-h})\) \({}+\operatorname{Cov}(Y_t,X_{t-h})\) \({}+\operatorname{Cov}(Y_t,Y_{t-h}).\)

Step 1Question 1 — Why the sum can fail to be stationary

The mean of \(Z_t\) is zero. However, stationarity of each process does not ensure that their cross-covariances depend only on the lag.

Take the example in the correction: \(Y_t=(-1)^tX_t\), with \(X_t\) zero-mean stationary and \(V(X_t)=\sigma_X^2>0\).

\[E(Y_t)=(-1)^tE(X_t)=0,\]\[V(Y_t)=(-1)^{2t}\sigma_X^2=\sigma_X^2,\]\[\operatorname{Cov}(Y_t,Y_{t-h})=(-1)^{2t-h}\gamma_X(h)\]\[=(-1)^h\gamma_X(h).\]

Thus \(Y_t\) is also stationary. But its covariance with \(X_t\) alternates:

\[\operatorname{Cov}(X_t,Y_t)=(-1)^t\sigma_X^2.\]
\[Z_t=X_t+Y_t=\begin{cases}2X_t, & t\text{ even},\\[3pt]0, & t\text{ odd},\end{cases}\]\[V(Z_t)=\begin{cases}4\sigma_X^2, & t\text{ even},\\[3pt]0, & t\text{ odd}.\end{cases}\]

No: the sum need not be stationary. Here its variance depends on whether \(t\) is even or odd.

The covariance calculation gives the same conclusion:

\[\operatorname{Cov}(Z_t,Z_{t-h})\]
\(=\bigl[1+(-1)^{t-h}+(-1)^t\) \({}+(-1)^{2t-h}\bigr]\gamma_X(h)\)
\[=[1+(-1)^t][1+(-1)^{t-h}]\gamma_X(h).\]
\[\operatorname{Cov}(Z_t,Z_{t-h})\]\[=\begin{cases}4\gamma_X(h), & t,h\text{ both even},\\[3pt]0, & \text{otherwise}.\end{cases}\]

Attention. This corrects the parity table in the supplied solution: the other three cases are zero.

Step 2Question 1 — When the processes are uncorrelated

Uncorrelated processes means \(\operatorname{Cov}(X_t,Y_s)=0\) for all dates \(t,s\). Both cross terms therefore vanish:

\[E(Z_t)=0,\]\[V(Z_t)=\gamma_X(0)+\gamma_Y(0),\]\[\operatorname{Cov}(Z_t,Z_{t-h})=\gamma_X(h)+\gamma_Y(h).\]

The sum is then second-order stationary. Its variance is constant and its covariance depends only on \(h\).

Step 3Question 2 — Moments of the linear process

FORMULA
\[E(U_t),\qquad V(U_t),\]\[\gamma_U(h)=\operatorname{Cov}(U_t,U_{t-h})\quad(h>0).\]

Use \(E(\varepsilon_t)=0\) and the fact that distinct white-noise terms are uncorrelated. The square-summability condition ensures the infinite sum has finite variance.

Expectation

\[E(U_t)=\mu+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\theta_kE(\varepsilon_{t-k})\]\[=\mu.\]

Variance

\[V(U_t)=V\!\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\theta_k\varepsilon_{t-k}\right)\]\[=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\theta_k^2V(\varepsilon_{t-k})\]\[=\sigma^2\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\theta_k^2<\infty.\]

Covariance at lag \(h>0\)

Center both observations at their common mean:

\[U_t-\mu=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\theta_j\varepsilon_{t-j},\]\[U_{t-h}-\mu=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\theta_k\varepsilon_{t-h-k}.\]
\[\gamma_U(h)=E[(U_t-\mu)(U_{t-h}-\mu)]\]\[=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\theta_j\theta_k E(\varepsilon_{t-j}\varepsilon_{t-h-k}).\]

Only matching innovation dates contribute:

\[t-j=t-h-k\quad\Longrightarrow\quad j=h+k.\]

For these terms, the expectation is \(\sigma^2\):

\[\gamma_U(h)=\sigma^2\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\theta_{k+h}\theta_k.\]
\[\boxed{E(U_t)=\mu}\]\[\boxed{V(U_t)=\sigma^2\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\theta_k^2}\]\[\boxed{\gamma_U(h)=\sigma^2\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\theta_k\theta_{k+h}}\]

The last expression holds for \(h>0\).
